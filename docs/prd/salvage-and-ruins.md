# PRD: Salvage & ruins

*Draft, 2026-10-13. Answers to [the vision](../vision.md).*

## Why this system exists

Parts are found, never crafted, so **salvage is the whole economy**. Every upgrade,
every new device and every better battery comes out of the sand. Salvage is also how
the moon tells its story: in what you find, in the order you find it, and in the hints
that someone else might still be out there.

**In the world.** The moon never made anything high-tech. It was an assembly-and-operations
economy at the far end of a manufacturing chain, so after the cut it could fit and repair
parts but never fabricate them. Stamps come at two levels: the assembler's mark on a whole
device, and each component's own maker inside it. Why a find is rare depends on why it's on the
moon: ordinary goods are common, goods only the hub ran on were only ever made in small
numbers, and contraband, such as a deserter's military kit, is rare because someone carried it
in. See [the lore research](../lore-research.md).

## What you find

- **Parts:** batteries, hooks, hover trays, panels, instruments. These are the things
  that fit sockets (Devices & mods).
- **Whole devices,** often with parts missing. You can haul one around (tray or rope)
  until you find what it needs, or strip it and fit its parts to your own devices.
- **Fuel:** rare consumables, and fissile material if you're very lucky (Power).
- **Seeds,** mostly in ruins (Farming), and in the Cistern order's seed vaults (proposed; see
  Seed vaults, below).
- **Survival kits:** a common find, usually holding seeds and water.
- **Lore:** fragments of what happened here.
- **Maker's marks:** almost everything found carries the mark of whoever made it, such as a
  government seal, a regulator's stamp, a shipyard logo, a contractor's tag, a comms
  casing or an improvised skimmer-crew patch. Commodity parts such as batteries carry none,
  so a mark means something where it appears. The player reconstructs the supply chain
  from salvage alone, with no exposition. The entities are in
  [the lore session](../lore-session-2026-10-14.md), and the makers beneath them in
  [part 2](../lore-session-2026-10-14-part-2.md).
- **Radio frequencies** to tune into (Radio, sound & atmosphere).
- **Water:** still open. How you get it and how you make it is a hard question that
  belongs to Survival.

## How finds show up

- **Half-buried in the sand.** Things stick out of the dunes, part-hidden. Burying is
  done by *placing* things partly below the surface, never by reshaping the ground
  (World & terrain, rule 2).
- **Ruins you can walk into.** These are structures sitting in the dunes, with sand
  drifted against their walls. Everything is above ground: there's no digging and
  nothing underground, bar Wow's resting place (Hunter, 2026-10-18, after session 5: the one thing
  allowed below, still unplaced).
- **Ruin types follow the terrain.** Skimmer cradles, tethers and catch stations stand on
  high ground, refineries cluster near volcanic vents, tank farms sit buried in basins,
  and port cradles and beacons stand on the heights. Gardens, water-cracking and power
  gather around the town sites. Improvised skimmer-crew wreckage is a separate, scrappier
  texture from the institutional ruins. Placing them needs the underlying topography
  (World & terrain), so it belongs to the full version.
- **Storms uncover things.** The harder the weather that exposed a place, the deeper
  the layer it reached.

## History runs backwards in the ground

The deeper the layer, the older the find, and the better its technology:

1. **Surface:** recent traces, and rare hints of holdouts.
2. **First Kingdoms to second age:** forged goods and cult works from the ages after the collapse (the
   First Kingdoms, the Dark Age and the second age), which share this layer. Session 4, part 5 reads
   the goods as older gear remade in the Dark Age ([below](#the-dark-age-as-a-lineage-rule-agreed-session-4-part-5-not-built)).
3. **Skimmer era:** skimmer-era salvage, in the middle.
4. **Colonial:** the original colonists' technology, which session 3 calls corporate tech.
   It's the oldest, the rarest and the best. You'll almost never find it in shallow erosion;
   it practically requires deep erosion. The best of it is **StarCom**, overbuilt for these
   exact conditions, but it turns up only as rare fragments (shards, relays and hardened
   modules), never a whole one.

**In the world (Agreed, session 3).** Corporate tech lies deepest and rarest, skimmer-era
salvage in the middle, and the forged goods of the First Kingdoms to the second age near the
surface. Dry, low-oxygen sand preserves ruins, and migrating dunes and storms expose and rebury
them, which is why harsher weather uncovers deeper loot. Burial needs little time: dunes and
eruptions bury ruins within centuries (session 4, part 2). The four layers above are these three
strata with a surface layer on top. How finds from the three ages differ inside their one layer
is not said, so this PRD doesn't try.

**Where structures stand (Agreed, session 4, part 4).** The newest session gives each era's structures
a burial depth of their own: colonial deepest and rarely exposed, First Kingdoms deep and barely visible,
skimmer era shallow and tilted, second age shallowest ([Structures by
era](#structures-by-era-and-the-loot-model-agreed-session-4-part-4-not-built)). That puts the skimmer
era above the First Kingdoms, where the layer list puts it below. This PRD follows the table for
structures and keeps the list as the skeleton's two-layer naming (open questions).

**The weather decides how deep the sand is stripped.** Stronger storms and fiercer
winds expose deeper layers. Venture into the storm, or travel into the wind, and you
find older, better things. Both difficulty dials pay out in salvage.

**Latitude gives two play styles.** The stormy equator is choppy and rich in salvage.
The calm poles have less loot, and nothing unique, but much more stability. That's where
you build big, with large solar farms and a thermal shield inside a wind shield, and
outpace the storms reliably.

Technically, a chunk's finds are placed when it loads, from the run's seed, the chunk's
coordinates and how hard the weather there has been. Like everything else, they're
stateless (World & terrain). Loot regenerating behind you isn't an exploit, because the
land ahead is always as rich.

### Two tiers, and the repeatable kit (Agreed direction)

There are two tiers: hand-designed **landmarks** (few, fixed per world; [Landmarks](#landmarks) below)
and a small **repeatable kit** per era that scatters across the world at zone-appropriate density.
"The kit is what to build first." Eras overlap: later sites sit on and reuse earlier ones. A kit is
three or four small, cheap pieces an era:

| Era | Kit pieces | Burial depth |
|---|---|---|
| Colonial | Pylon stubs, pipeline segments, hab pods, relay masts | Deepest; rarely exposed |
| Skimmer era | Wreck hulls, fuel bladders, dugouts | Shallow, at an angle; exposed and reburied fast |
| First Kingdoms (first age) | Pipe-wall huts, cisterns, low stone walls | Deep; barely visible |
| Second age | Wayshrines, watchtowers, house ruins | Shallowest; most often exposed |

Each piece has **three exposure states: buried, partly exposed, exposed.** Storms shift them. "A
reburied piece can come back later with fresh loot."

### What each era left (Agreed)

**Colonial (oldest).** The best infrastructure loot. Landmarks: Port Morgan (spaceport pads,
warehouses, Harbourmaster offices, Tilg terminals), the Halloran refinery terraces, Starcom
(high-altitude comms), the observatory and the orbital launch site. Generated pieces: vent power
stations along the rift; buried hab blocks and tunnel networks (vat fungus, glowcap, office fern);
Frontier Ag farm domes and seed stores; water-import aqueducts, pump stations and tanks; survey
beacons and relay towers on a grid; frontier towns on the plains. *Encounter:* big, deep, mostly
buried. A storm strips a dune and exposes a roof or tower top, so it is the same structure with a
different way in each time. Dig or find a breach; the best loot is deepest.

**Skimmer era.** A short era with little architecture of its own: mostly **scars on colonial
structures.** Wreck hulls, improvised landing pads with fuel bladders, fortified deserter dugouts with
weapons caches, black-site checkpoints and patrol posts around the rift, and squatter habs patched with
scrap. *Encounter:* half-buried at an angle, and the wind exposes and reburies them fast. Loot it now.

**First Kingdoms (the first age).** Part 4 described it as "survivors on failing colonial power, then
war"; part 5 says that was wrong and "for first-age detail, part 2 is canon." So this is part 2's age:
three powers at constant war, with feuds inside each, whose kings, guilds and barons are keepers of
working machines nobody can build any more, and a barony is a well, a turbine, a dig site or a stretch
of road. Part 4's pieces stand, read as the keepers' and the common folk's: pipe towns built into
colonial pipelines; generator shrines around the last working generators (possibly the first sacred
sites, part 2's "neutral holy sites"); cut-and-cover halls roofed with hull plates; cisterns where
colonial water lines ended (before the Sisters); low walls and old sandroot field plots; buried war
earthworks. Part 2's pieces are the other half, as readings of its places and not a kit the session
lists: Halloran's refinery terraces as a citadel with a single crossing for its gate, the lower
city beneath it, road towns that charge tolls, salvage towns on big ruins, and the great houses'
holdings in Port Morgan, with heraldry and guild stamps on what they hold. *Encounter:* low stone
foundations, found by surface signs (saltbush lines, ancient sandroot). Digging reveals a floor plan.
More likely than second-age sites to hold working colonial tech.

**Second age (most common).** Kingdoms slowly dying of thirst, so most structures revolve around
water. Wayshrines (small Cistern shrines on pilgrim roads: basins, offerings, a few seeds); stepwells
(stairs down to dry bottoms, with dewleaf and maybe stormcup in the shade); ration gates (fortified
checkpoints where the Sisters controlled water: lockboxes, tokens); caravanserais (walled rest stops
at day-long intervals on the artery); watchtowers (tall, the most visible landmarks, with late-war
weapons caches); granaries (sealed stores of preserved seed or salt); walled villages (the bulk of
sites, built into colonial ruins in Mon); battlefields (late wars, around Halfrise). *Zones:* the Rim
is densest (sacked Halfrise, intact and empty Sister, shrines, ration gates and stepwells between).
Mon has villages in colonial ruins, granaries and salvage yards. Han has Muster, the Crossing and
Aitch, and little else. *Encounter:* tall and fairly intact; towers stand out of the dunes.

### Two channels (Agreed)

- **Drift.** Storms carry light debris, scraps and occasional small items across the map. It is
  caught as it blows past, perhaps with a net or tether mod. Steady, low value, and it rewards a good
  camp. **Terrain matters:** a camp in a sheltered valley below a dune crest where high winds ramp
  over should be one of the best passive farms.
- **Expedition.** Big storms reshape dunes and expose ruins. Afterward, go explore before the sites
  rebury. This is where the good loot is, and it pulls you away from camp.

The storm loop: shelter and catch drift while it rages, then go into its wake to hunt what it
uncovered. "Weak players avoid storms, strong players hunt them, and everyone gets caught."

### Exposure window (Agreed)

Relaxed, not a hard timer. Sites rebury gradually. A returning storm can expose a **deeper layer** of
the same ruin, so revisiting pays.

### Loot identity by era (Agreed)

| Era | Best for |
|---|---|
| Colonial | Infrastructure: power, chassis, big systems |
| Skimmer era | **Best single-item gear:** military weapons, armour, mods from deserters and the black site |
| First Kingdoms (first age) | Intact working colonial tech used in new ways |
| Dark Age | No sites, so no loot of its own: it is the lineage of the second age's gear (below) |

### Surface signs

Saltbush lines, glow in cracks, coalcane groves and ancient sandroot all say something is buried
beneath ([Farming](farming.md#propagation-growth-stages-and-wild-habitats-agreed)).

### Finding them from afar (Hunter, 2026-10-18: "we will need to give the player a way to actually find them early now that the desert is barren")

With loot gathered into sites and open sand nearly empty, a site has to be findable by eye,
with no map or marker:
- **Seen from far off.** What stands tall (relay masts, watchtowers, pylon stubs, a wreck's hull)
  shows on the horizon from kilometres away, and exposed metal or glass glints when the sun
  catches it, so a glint on a far dune is worth walking to.
- **Roads lead to sites.** A saltbush line, a row of pylon stubs or a run of old pipe reads from
  a distance as a line across the dunes, and following it brings you to what stands along it.
- **A site in sight at every start.** Each origin begins with something to walk to in view: the
  Salvager's wrecks, the Homesteader's wayshrine, and for the Surveyor a site on the horizon.

#### What's built (2026-10-18, the `sightlines` branch)

One system draws what you see of history past the built ground: the skyline (`Skyline`,
`game/world/skyline.gd`, under the terrain). Each time the sheet follows you, a worker makes what's in

### Readings, not in the session

- **The strata and the table don't agree about two eras.** Session 3's strata, which "History runs
  backwards in the ground" above rests on, put corporate tech deepest, skimmer-era salvage in the
  middle and the ages after the collapse near the surface. The table puts the skimmer era shallow and
  the First Kingdoms deep. They can both hold if the strata are about where loot lay when the sand began
  and the table is about how deep a standing structure sits now (a wreck stands up out of the sand,
  a low stone foundation doesn't), but it is a reading, and this PRD follows the table for
  structures (open questions).
- **Exposure can be a function of the weather, as finds are.** The built finds are laid from the seed,
  the chunk and "how hard the weather has been there," read over the last two hours. A structure's
  state (buried, partly exposed, exposed) can be read the same way when its chunk is made, so a storm
  that has passed gives a different state to a chunk loaded after it, and nothing under your feet is
  reshaped (rule 2). A state that changes while you stand there is another matter (open questions).
  *Since 2026-10-18 it does, smoothly, as a storm works the site, and never over you
  ([Wind & storms](wind-and-storms.md#storms-with-a-payoff-agreed-2026-10-18-spec)).*
- **Drift answers the collector's question.** The notes' collector "catches wind-blown loot. What
  blows is open." Part 4 says what: light debris, scraps and occasional small items. That narrows the
  question and does not settle how it sits with finds that are "frozen where they lie."
- **The lee is the drift farm.** "A sheltered valley below a dune crest where high winds ramp over" is
  the lee of a dune ([Wind & storms](wind-and-storms.md#the-lee-of-a-dune)), where the wind is weakest
  and a camp could collect what falls out of it. The session does not say it is the same thing.
- **The kit pieces are two sizes of ruin.** The built ruin is a roofed building with a hall. A
  wayshrine, a watchtower and a house ruin are three smaller or taller pieces of the same kind, each
  with a loot table, and the step that builds them replaces the notes' base case of "pillars and wall
  stubs."

## Wrecks and scrapping (Hunter, 2026-10-18; built)

*Hunter's direction, 2026-10-18: "embed loot into larger structures out in the sand, and require a
variety of operations to remove valuable parts from a wreck. Operations like removing outer plating
with a heat cutting tool or excavating deeper into the sand to expose more of the wreck." Optional
operations too: "things that don't have to be done to get something out of the wreck but the player
pays a price for not being prepared enough to do," like "a biohazard or electrical hazard, forcing
the player to take an injury." "All wrecks should have multiple salvageable pieces with multiple
physics interactable modules attached to them. Wrecks themselves will need to be made as preset large
structures, made to reflect the moon's past." On excavating: "No digging under the surface. No
digging at all really. More like structures that are full of sand after sitting in sandstorms for so
long." The prototype (the `scrapper` branch) built it beside the old sites; after playing it Hunter
asked for it rebuilt on the world system and polished, with five wrecks, plates cut by tracing their
seams, the shock and the sickness, and cut plates as scrap for now (2026-10-18).*

Built on the `wrecks` branch. "No digging and nothing underground" holds: the sand you clear is packed
inside a wreck, above the ground, and nothing is dug out of the dunes.

- **Five wrecks, each a kit piece history lays** (`WreckKit`, `game/salvage/kits/wrecks.gd`), standing,

- **Cut.** A plate is welded over an opening at its seams, a bead along each edge. Hold use with a heat
  cutter (`Cutter`) and its hot spot lands where you aim; on a seam it cuts the weld, the cells under it
  and either side of it, at 1.5 s a metre of seam times the wreck's armour (Tuning `cut_seconds_per_m`;
  traced cell by cell it takes about two thirds of that, since the kerf runs ahead). Off the seam it does
  nothing and says to trace the weld. The cutter draws 120 W from its own battery while it fires, its
  beam reaching from its nozzle to the hot spot, sparks flying where it bites. A cell just cut glows
  white, cooling through orange to a dark kerf; the plate warms round it. Every seam cut, the plate comes
  away and falls outward, clangs as it lands, and lies where it fell, for good. A seam under the dunes'
  sand can't be cut: a buried or partly exposed wreck keeps some plates until a storm strips it.
- **Clear.** A hold packed with storm sand clears in slices from its open end, with a scoop held on the
  open slice, 0.35 s a cubic metre (Tuning `clear_seconds_per_m3`); the slice wears back as you scoop, a
  stroke and a spray of sand at a time. A hold cleared down from the top lies in level layers whatever
  the wreck's lean. Packed sand shows the thin lines of the storms that laid it.
- **Modules** are whole devices, held fast where they were stowed (on racks or floors) until what's in
  their way is gone: each lists the plates and the slices of sand it needs. Taking one, or using it,
  says why it won't come ("behind the starboard hatch · cut it away", "packed in sand · clear the cargo
  bay"). They come from the wreck kind's own rows (`SiteLoot`'s `module` and `bay` sockets): the skimmer
  era's single items, the Provisional's military kit, colonial freight, a lifeboat's survival stores, the
  Knights' convoy gear; every module is there, with at least two parts fitted where it has sockets for
  them. Once free it's an ordinary find. Every wreck holds three or four, and its other sockets hold
  loot as any site's do.
- **Hazards, the optional operations.** A **live capacitor bank** rides on some modules: lay a **stake**
  across its terminals (use, with the stake in your hands) and it shorts out in a burst of sparks, the
  stake falling where it was, yours still. A **breached store** rides on others: use **sealant** on it,
  and the canister is used up. Take the module without, and the hazard goes off on you once:
  - **Shock:** 25 health at once, a white flash and a crack, thrown off the module (you don't take it
    that grab, and what you held drops), stunned for 1.5 s, unable to walk, look round or close your
    hands. The bank is spent; the module comes the next time.
  - **Sickness:** 0.08 health a second for 240 s of world time, thirst 1.8 times faster, no healing, the
    edges of your sight sallow while it lasts. A **med kit** cures it, used in your hands or from what
    you carry. A save keeps it.
  A live bank blinks red and crackles; a breached store hisses and seeps a sickly mist.
- **The readout** says what each piece is, how far its work has got and what does it ("starboard hatch ·
  seams 72% cut · hold use with a heat cutter along its seams", "packed sand · cargo bay, blown in

- **Storms work wrecks too** (2026-10-18, on master's live exposure: [What storms do to sites
  now](../world-system.md#what-storms-do-to-sites-now)). A wreck rises out of the sand and sinks back as a
  storm works it, and its scrapping goes with it frame by frame: its plates, hazards and packed holds
  stand at its floor as its chunk draws it (`Wreckage.follow`, from `Salvage.follow`), and its modules
  are read again as any site's finds are. A storm moves no sand on a wreck's ground (no drift, and its
  rooms fill as far as it sinks), so burying one is its sinking. What's cut stays cut and what's cleared
  stays cleared, under the sand or bared, since the storm's work and the wreck's memory are apart: one
  is the weather's function, the other what you did. Each 3 cm it moves, the sand is asked again of its
  seams (a buried seam can't be cut, a bared one can) and its holds packed from the top are laid again on
  the sand in the hull, so a cargo deck under the sand when its ground was built has sand to clear once
  a storm bares it. **A cut plate stays where it fell, and is buried with the wreck:** one lying on the
  wreck itself (its lowest corner over 10 cm above the sand: a pod's top, a deck) rides it; one on the
  sand sinks with the wreck as a storm buries it deeper than when the plate fell, out of sight, and
  comes back up to where it fell as it's bared, but never rises off the sand it lies on as the wreck
  rises past that. The hold over you is the site's own, so it holds the wreck and its plates alike, and
  a save keeps the plates' rule with them (`fallen`: where each came to rest in the wreck's frame, and
  how far the wreck was sunk then if it lies on the sand).
- **Memory.** What was cut, cleared and made safe, and where each plate fell, is kept with the site's
  memory of what was taken (`Salvage`, the site's id): built again as it was left whenever its ground
  is, and in a save once worked (an untouched wreck saves nothing). Once the sheet lifts it, it's
  forgotten, like any site.

## What Sand Game taught us

Walk-in ruins had collision trouble, for the same reasons the terrain did:

- **Ruins were placed at a fixed height** (8–14 m above the chunk's origin), ignoring
  the dunes entirely, so they floated or sank.
- **Collision came straight from the imported model,** which gives thin, one-sided
  surfaces.
- **Each ruin was scaled up at random by 2–3.5×,** collision included.
- **Loot spawned at the ruin's own center,** inside the walls, frozen until you came
  near.

## Rules

1. **Ruins are part of the ground's one function.** A ruin's footprint is built into
   `height_at`, so the sand meets its floor and drifts up its walls exactly. Its
   position comes from the same seed and coordinates as the terrain around it.
2. **Ruin collision is simple shapes** (boxes and slabs), built for walking: floors,
   walls and doorways. Never collision taken from a detailed mesh.
3. **Nothing physical is ever scaled at runtime.** Different sizes are different pieces.
4. **Ruins never move.** They're static, never physics objects.
5. **Finds are placed, not dropped.** Every find sits at its intended depth: on the
   sand, partly buried, or on a ruin's floor. Loose finds settle from a few centimetres,
   never from metres.
6. **Finds are typed parts and devices** (Devices & mods), so anything salvaged works
   with your sockets the moment you pick it up.

## Probes

1. **Deterministic.** The same seed, chunk and weather always give the same finds.
2. **Finds sit where they should.** Every find rests at its intended depth, never
   floating and never buried out of reach. Loose finds settle without falling through.
3. **Ruins sit in the sand.** Every ruin floor meets the sand within tolerance.
4. **The walk-through.** A probe walks every doorway and floor of every ruin type. The
   player never snags, never gets stuck, and never falls through. *Since 2026-10-18 it's
   the structure kits' probe 2 (`probes/kits/`), which walks every kind with a way in.*
5. **Harder weather, better finds.** Across many chunks, the average find tier rises
   with storm and wind exposure.
6. **Strip and fit.** Every part in a found device can be taken out and fitted to one of
   yours.
7. **Nothing leaks.** On the 20 km walk, the number of finds in memory stays bounded.
8. **Out the door.** A found device, once yours, can be taken hold of and walked out of
   a ruin's doors, and towed out of them on a line.

Wrecks have their own seven, in `probes/wrecks/` ([Wrecks and scrapping](#wrecks-and-scrapping-hunter-2026-10-18-built)).

## Out of scope

- Digging, and anything underground, bar Wow's resting place (Hunter, 2026-10-18).
- NPCs, trading and crafting. (A device auto-crafting its own recipes, such as Mitchell's module, is an
  Idea and no player crafting.)

## Lore

Lore starts in **item descriptions**. They hint and never state. The game never says
outright what happened here. In the world, spelling carries age: X-spelled names
(XN-118-7, Xanathivin) are old and official, and the Z-spelled Zan is late and colloquial
([the lore research](../lore-research.md#names-the-ug-catalog-and-the-moon)). Session 3 adds
that the forms between them, skimmer-era slang and the variants of the ages after the collapse,
date a ruin too, and that the giant's name, Tu, wears down the same way from a designation
number ([the names](../lore-research.md#hierarchy-and-the-name-decay-agreed-session-3)).
Session 4, part 2 adds worn names and slang to use in carvings and radio chatter: Zan, Tu, Han,
Mon, Aitch, Monward, Hanmen, Zanborn and the tu- prefix
([the names](../lore-research.md#slang-and-name-erosion-agreed-session-4-part-2)).

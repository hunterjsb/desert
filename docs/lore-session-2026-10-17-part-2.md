# Desert — Lore Session, 17 Oct 2026 (part 2)

Oct 17, 2026 · @Hunter Boyd

## How to use this

This is a supplement to the 16 Oct lore doc, the 17 Oct session notes and the Tu sky spec. Merge it into the corpus. Where it contradicts existing docs, this is the newer decision; flag the conflict and keep this version unless Hunter says otherwise.

The game has no NPCs and no exposition. Everything here reaches the player through marks, carvings, ruin layering, terminals, radio and the sky. Most of it is iceberg; depth markers are noted where they were decided.

## Renames and retcons

- **No more "Bronze Age".** The post-collapse societies are medieval in shape (Kenshi-like post-apocalyptic feudal), not Bronze Age. Use **First Kingdoms**, **Dark Age** and **Second Age**. Replace the term everywhere, including tier names and comments.
- **Shorter timeline.** The 10–15k-year post-collapse span is gone. The player arrives roughly **two thousand years** after the collapse. Burial doesn't need the time: dunes and eruptions bury ruins within centuries.
- **First Kingdoms keep their knowledge.** Port Morgan had unbroken generations and offline archives, so nobody forgot how machines work. What's lost is the ability to **manufacture** alloys, chips and catalysts. Real knowledge loss and machine worship only arrive in the Dark Age.
- **Tilg & Standard.** Long after the war, TILG merged with its old rival Standard Freight. The surviving company is **Tilg & Standard**. TILG has worn into "Tilg": same letters, nobody remembers it was an acronym.
- **The Z on surface crates stays.** The hand-daubed "ZN" consignment mark is intentional drift from XN.

## Tu, tidal lock and water

Zan is tidally locked to Tu at any orbit we've considered, so Tu hangs in a fixed spot on the near side and never rises on the far side. The sky defines the map.

| Region | Tu in the sky | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under Tu (near side) | High, fixed | Longest noon eclipses, Tu-shine at night, hottest. Colonial heartland, richest ruins. Dries first. |
| The Rim | On the horizon, half-risen | No eclipse, mild, ash-rich soil, keeps water longest on the near side. Farmland and both empires' heartland. |
| Far side | Never visible | Black nights, stars only, cold. Water ends up here. Frontier, exile, "where the god can't see you". |

- **Orbit history.** Zan's orbit was more eccentric when the colonists came, driving tidal heating and volcanism. It has been rounding out since, so heating fades and the vents go quiet. With no fresh outgassing, the atmosphere slowly leaks away: the world's clock.
- **Tu's size.** Keep angular size a tuning value; test 6°, 9° and 13° in game before fixing the orbit. Physics follows the look.
- **Water migration.** The near side is heated by the sun and by Tu's infrared; the far side runs colder. Water evaporates on the warm side and cold-traps as frost and ground ice on the far side. While the vents outgassed they topped the near side up; once they quieted, the near side drained outward. Gameplay hook: condensers work better toward the far side.
- **Weather hooks (proposed, not settled):** an eclipse squall, air rushing toward Tu's noon shadow; and a slow storm season when Zan swings closest and Tu looks visibly larger.

## Geography

The three great sites lie on one rough line running from the Rim in toward the point under Tu:

1. **Halfrise**, on the Rim, Tu half-risen on the horizon. Second Age capital.
2. **Port Morgan**, partway in on its plateau. Tu high but off to one side: Port Morgan is not dead centre, which is why one stretch of the Rim (the stretch closest to it) matters far more than the rest.
3. **Halloran**, deepest in, near the sub-Tu point. Rift valley; refinery on the terraces, H-Town on the floor. Hottest, longest eclipse, first to dry and first to lose its vents.

The endgame is a journey down this axis. Frontier towns sit on the road between Port Morgan and Halloran.

## Eras after the collapse

Years are approximate and counted from the collapse; only their order and rough scale are decided.

| Era | Approx. years | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collapse | 0–100 | City-states cling to working machines; mass starvation and infant mortality. |
| First Kingdoms | \~100 to several centuries | Three powers at constant war; knowledge kept, manufacturing lost. |
| The fall | End of First Kingdoms | Vents migrate out of reach, near side dries, feudal wars plus a second famine. |
| Dark Age | Unfixed | Survivors scatter; archives and guilds lost; machine worship takes root. |
| Second Age | Peaks \~1,500 years in | Rim empire around Halfrise; crusade for Port Morgan; esoteric AI cults. |
| Decline | Late Second Age onward | The moon can't sustain cities; nomads take over, then nearly vanish. |
| Player arrives | \~2,000 years | Region recovering; Zan being rediscovered by surveyors, salvagers and homesteaders. |

## First Kingdoms

The core rule of the era: **power belongs to whoever holds the working machines.** Kings, guilds and barons are keepers of tech nobody can build any more. Every repair cannibalises something else, which is where reworked, low-power gear comes from: clever adaptation, not superstition.

Three powers, always raiding each other, never merging or making lasting peace. Inside each, Game of Thrones-style family conflict.

- **Port Morgan** (the Morgan kingdom): the dominant power. The crown passes between great families over centuries:
  - **Haberjack:** descended from the Harbourmaster who stayed; claims the crown by right of the port.
  - **Pellicott:** IOU district bosses turned lords; dockworker blood, popular.
  - **Crake:** the engineer line from the colonial technicians; keepers of the archives and machines (and later the Ledger).
  - **Vonatelli:** merchants controlling water and grain trade with the Rim; they switch sides.
- **Halloran:** the refinery terraces become the citadel; H-Town is the lower city. Same escarpment, same single crossing, now a castle gate. Has geothermal power and the old security force's muscle.
- **The Rim Kingdom:** a label the cities use, not a real state. Loose, disconnected farm towns and co-ops, maybe tied by a council or market fair. Holds the food but can never agree, so the big two buy off different farms.
- **Free towns and barons:** road towns that flip allegiance and charge tolls, salvage towns on big ruins, neutral holy sites. A baron is whoever holds a well, a turbine, a dig site or a stretch of road; lose the machine, lose the title.

**Religion** is emerging but not yet dominant (see Faith).

**The fall.** The vents didn't die, they **moved**, and nobody had the rigs to follow the heat. Halloran goes dark, the near side keeps drying, and the kingdoms fight over the Rim's water and food. Feudal war plus a second famine breaks all three.

## Dark Age and Second Age

**Dark Age.** Survivors scatter; barely anyone makes it. The archives and engineer guilds don't survive, so this is where knowledge is truly lost. The few who learn to live sustainably off the moon, following water and heat, seed what comes next.

**Second Age.** One dominant empire rises from the Rim towns, centred on the stretch of Rim closest to Port Morgan. Everyone wants a Pax Romana; in practice it's Holy Roman Empire-style: a crown everyone nominally swears to, with one or more city-states rebelling at any time. External enemies are less defined than in the First Kingdoms.

- **Capital: Halfrise**, at the head of the old road in to Port Morgan. Named for Tu half-risen on the horizon. Port Morgan snobs call it "Half Price".
- **Mother Cistern's temple** sits in a sister city near Halfrise (unnamed).
- **Settlement pattern.** The good cities are all on the Rim. Further in toward Tu, settlements get stranger, living on what they own rather than what they grow: salvage towns, shrines and bot cults, caravan stops on water routes. All depend on the Rim for food. Nomads range everywhere.
- **The crusade for Port Morgan.** The empire can't reliably hold the old capital, so holding it is a Templar/Crusades-style holy war, kept by a military order with water convoys and fortified outposts dug into colonial ruins. Gameplay: crusader bunkers sit on top of much older sites.

**Port Morgan's ruins (Mon)** are a buried megacity, like medieval Rome inside its old walls: separate enclaves in an uninhabited zone, all technically the holy city, all quarrelling.

| Enclave | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| The Bastion | The military order's fortress around the Ledger's mainframe. |
| Crakeside | Salvage quarter in the old IOU freight districts, named for the fallen Pellicotts. Diggers and black market. |
| The Pads | Settlement on the old landing field; caravans and traders. |
| Lowtown | Squatters in half-buried districts, sand to the windows. Poor and independent. |

**Halloran's ruins (Han)** support three settlements:

| Settlement | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Muster | The Quartermaster's war cult on the old refinery terraces above the black site; named for stencilled "muster point" signs. |
| The Crossing | Trade and toll stop at the old lift and switchback between terraces and floor. |
| Aitch | Salvage town digging the buried rift floor; the last fossil of "H-Town". |

**Frontier salvage towns** on the old road between Mon and Han: **Pumpwell** (around a long-dead colonial water pump) and **Saloon** (a surviving bar sign nobody can read any more).

## Faith and the AI gods

**The faith of Tu** emerges in the First Kingdoms and dominates the Second Age, where it grows stronger and stranger.

- Tu is **the Eye**: it watches and never blinks, except at noon. The noon eclipse is the daily rite, predicted to the second.
- The drying is read as judgement; walking toward the far side is leaving the god's sight.
- **The hive mind is original sin, not the devil.** A line in the oldest scripture about the thing that brought the fall, recited and never thought about. Closer to the apple than to Morgoth. Bottom of the iceberg.
- Marks: First Kingdoms gear carries heraldry and guild stamps; Second Age gear carries religious symbols.

**The AI gods.** Local models that survived the collapse. They speak only through terminals, never as characters. They were built to coordinate but have no link to each other any more, so their machine-to-machine traffic is carried on foot by priests who can't read it: a supply request becomes scripture, a reply becomes a war. Many Second Age conflicts are bad translations between AIs.

| AI | Origin | Where | Worship |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Ledger | Tilg logistics model; so capable it's used for everything. Probably the archive the Crakes kept. | Mainframe in Port Morgan's ruins (the Bastion). | Everyone wants a piece of it; the crusade is really about it. It knows the ships, cargo and routes. |
| The Quartermaster | The Provisional State's military AI. | The black site, a bunker cut into the rift wall near Halloran, kept alive by geothermal power. | Patron of the military order and of Muster's war cult. Holds military clearances. |
| Mother Cistern | Condenser-plant AI, possibly with the colony clinic's records. | Temple in a city near Halfrise. | An order of priestesses: healing, fertility, water and rain. Its miracles really work. |
| The Walker | A legged survey robot that never stops mapping. | Wandering. | Nomad pilgrims trail it across the desert. |

**The military order** guards the Ledger for everyone but kneels to the Quartermaster. It recruits from cities all across the Rim, which is why rebels can't touch it. Its knights are guards, not interpreters. Built-in tension: if the Quartermaster's orders clash with what the Ledger's priests want, the holy city has a problem. **Name not settled**; current favourite is the **Knights Requisitioner** (crusader title, secretly a supply form).

## Nomads and the moving city

The Second Age cities eventually fail: the moon can't sustain them. Nomad tribes become the prevailing way of life, then nearly die out too.

- **What the player finds is mostly old.** Waymarks pointing to water, caches and camp scars belong to dead tribes. A nomad radio relay still broadcasts storm and water reports in Zan-Han-Mon slang on automatic, a ghost station long after its builders died.
- **One nomad city survives.** It moves: a huge tethered raft of salvaged chassis, shields and turbines drifting with the water and outrunning storms, the player's own mechanics scaled to a civilisation. Rarer than the AI gods; near the bottom of the iceberg, above only the hive mind.
- **Shown only in silhouette.** Back-lit decks and figures, never faces or words.

## Landmarks and endings

The game reads as an infinite survival game. Endings exist only for players who choose them for story reasons; many players will never see one.

**Landmarks** are one general system, not one-offs: a small class of prefab sites at fixed coordinates on the planet grid, the same in every run, that persist the player's changes. They're the only fixed things on a shifting moon, the exception to unloading out-of-range content. They can unlock endings and, for the prisoner, spawns. Candidates: the StarCom antenna, the black site, the Bastion and the Ledger.

| Ending | How | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| StarCom | Repair the StarCom interstellar antenna with the right parts. | Comms are instant but ships take years: Tilg answers, extraction is years out, so the finale is surviving until they arrive. |
| Launch | In Port Morgan, locate an old ship, fuel and power it, and connect the Ledger and the Quartermaster so both authorise. | The Ledger knows the ships; the Quartermaster holds the clearances. Connecting them means bridging Mon and Han across open desert. Two gods talking directly for the first time in centuries. |
| Shot down | Launch with only the Ledger's approval. | A two-thousand-year-old air-defence net shoots down an unauthorised launch. Shot down by paperwork. |
| Nomad city | Survive long enough with a camp worth finding: powered shield, turbines, radio on the right frequency. | The city finds you; a tether line lands at camp and pulls you in. Silhouettes only. Giga-rare; best fit for oysters. |
| Radio | Find the right frequencies to reach Tilg or the last town. | Loose idea, not designed. |

**Running gags to keep.** The Ledger is Tilg property, so the surveyor is the only person on Zan with a login to the god of the crusades. The Quartermaster is the Provisional's, so a Tilg login means nothing to it.

**Hive mind start changes the endings without a cutscene.** Fix StarCom and someone answers, but not Tilg. Try to launch and the Quartermaster recognises you as the enemy, whatever your clearance. No dedicated hive mind ending; it stays ambiguous.

## Slang and name erosion

Names wear down over time; the short forms are roots for Second Age words.

| Original | Worn form |
| --- | --- |
| XN-118-7 | Zan |
| XN-118-2 (the giant) | Tu |
| Halloran (the region) | Han |
| Port Morgan (the region) | Mon |
| H-Town | Aitch |
| TILG | Tilg |
| Harbourmaster (TILG post) | Civic ruler's title after the war; later royal |

Derived slang: **Monward** (the road to the holy city), **Hanmen** (people from the deep rift, said with suspicion on the Rim), **Zanborn** (natives, versus newcomers), and **tu-** as a prefix for the sacred or fixed: tu-noon (the eclipse), tu-shadow (its cold spot). Use these in carvings and radio chatter.

# Lore session, part 2: makers, the harbor, the far end, and the names

*Session notes, 2026-10-14 (Hunter + Claude, voice, continued). Companion to
[lore-session-2026-10-14.md](lore-session-2026-10-14.md). This is direction Hunter agreed to
in the session, not final canon until he says so. Where something is a Claude proposal Hunter
hasn't picked yet, it's marked **(proposal)**.*

## 1. The OEM tier: who actually makes things

Part 1's entity stack is all operators, regulators and integrators: people who *deploy*
things. Nobody in it *manufactures* anything. The Remote Moon Contractor sets up and maintains
infrastructure until the colony reaches steady state, then TILG runs it until the town takes
on a life of its own. Neither one fabricates the core technology. They buy it.

So there's a tier underneath everything: **OEMs**, the hardware manufacturers back in
civilisation who build the core components everyone else resells, integrates and rebrands.

- **Issuer vs. maker.** The integrator's mark is on the *assembly*; the OEM's mark is on the
  *component*. The player finds a shield whose housing says Remote Moon Contractor; open it
  and the emitter core carries a manufacturer most settlers never heard of.
- **The box.** A shipping crate is addressed to an operator in town (the Port Authority, a big
  business). Inside, every part carries its own manufacturer's stamp. The address label says
  who *ordered* it; the contents say who *made* it.
- **Hardware rhymes with the minds.** Physical gear now has the same layered provenance as
  the AI models in part 1: OEM core → integrator housing → operator deployment → local owner.
  Same structure, two media. Players feel the consistency without being told.
- **Why the colony couldn't recover.** The moon was always an assembly-and-operations economy
  at the far end of a manufacturing chain it couldn't reproduce. After the cut it can
  integrate and repair, never fabricate. The fabs were never here.

## 2. Three kinds of rarity

A reusable rule for salvage, set by *why* an item is on the moon at all:

| Category | Why it's here | Why it's rare (or not) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial OEM** | Normal procurement | Common. Mass-made, deployed everywhere. | Starting shield, solar, wind |
| **Industrial / infrastructural** | Legitimately here because the hub ran on it | Rare **by fleet size**: there were only ever a few. | Freighter He-3 cores, port machinery |
| **Off-supply-chain** | Shouldn't be here at all | Rare **by contraband**: someone brought it. Every piece carries a story. | Deserters' military gear, smuggled goods |

Off-supply-chain gear is rare, powerful and narrative all at once, from one fact: military
tech had no business on a civilian transit hub. The only way it exists is that deserters
fled the war carrying it.

## 3. Shields

The ladder is built entirely from provenance. There are no arbitrary tier numbers; the power
curve *is* the economy.

- **Main shield OEM.** The common shield and the player's starting shield. Found all over the
  world.
- **Competing OEM.** Same tier, a different design philosophy, so it's a real tradeoff rather
  than a reskin (e.g. lighter vs. tougher). **(proposal: the specific tradeoff)**
- **Off-meta specialists.** A couple of small makers with niche builds.
- **The military shield.** Government-issue, off the supply chain, brought by one of the
  handful of deserters who lived among generations of colonists. Blocks **both heat and dust**,
  which commercial shields can't do together. Very rare, very strong.

Names are not chosen yet. "Acme Shield Corporation" is a placeholder for the main OEM.

## 4. Power

- **Batteries are anonymous.** Commodity infrastructure, like screws or rope, so no logo. This
  keeps maker's marks meaningful: they only appear where they carry signal.
- **Solar.** A big commodity OEM. Everywhere, cheap, useless in storms. Common starting power
  because everyone had it.
- **Wind.** A different maker. Planted gear, strong in storms, ties you down. Encodes the
  movement-vs-power tradeoff from vision.md.
- **Possible mid-tier.** A better cell, or a deserter's compact field reactor (the power
  equivalent of the military shield). **(proposal)**
- **Apex: the freighter He-3 core.** Not a stationary refinery: a solo player can't *operate* a
  facility, and that fights the fantasy. Instead, a compact He-3 reactor pulled from the dead
  core of a wrecked freighter. A component you extract and carry, not a plant you tend.
  - It's industrial, not military and not consumer: a freighter engine. There are only ever a
    few thousand freighters in service across the whole lane network, so there are only ever a
    few cores. Scarcity by fleet size.
  - Any serious ship runs He-3, which is fuel and wealth in one substance ("gaseous gold"). If a
    line *isn't* running He-3, there had better be a damn good reason it's paying to supplement.
  - The engine that made the colony possible becomes the player's endgame battery.
  - **(proposal)** The core is stamped by the *shipwright* (the spaceship builder from the
    part 1 stack), not a power company. The player has walked past that logo on hull wreckage
    all game.

## 5. Freighters are moving towns

**No FTL in Xandaris** (or, if FTL ever exists, this history predates it). Lane crossings take
years.

- A freighter isn't a truck; it's a **town that travels**, with its own districts, hierarchy
  and population living aboard for years at a stretch. Comparable in size to a spaceport town.
- A **freighter wreck is the ruin of a small mobile city.** The biggest, most dangerous, most
  rewarding dig sites in the game: a dungeon with its He-3 core at the bottom.

## 6. The port city at peak

The reason the hub grew: after years in transit, freighter-towns need somewhere to resupply,
repair and let their people off. **The moon is a harbor for mobile cities**, not a gas station.

- **Its rhythm is tidal.** When a freighter-town docks, crews who've been aboard for years
  pour out, and the place swells with money and chaos. When the lane is quiet, a skeleton crew
  tends the garden and the vents.
- **Settlement pattern so far:** one big port city, refinery settlements near the vents, and
  possibly a secondary minor port.
- **Still open:** do docked crews mix into town (a transient quarter, bars, markets, strangers
  who leave again) or stay aboard and trade through the port? Rough peak population? Daily life
  in the good window? This is the next session: it's the era the player walks through the
  corpse of, so it's the most surfaced lore left to build.

## 7. Names: the UG catalog and the moon

### The United Government's designation system

- **Hierarchical: sector, system, body.** Hierarchical codes scale forever and encode
  *location*, not just identity. A designation is a coordinate.
- **XN is the sector prefix** for Xandar/Xandaris. Non-negotiable.
- The UG is sprawling and administratively thin, so designations should read as one line in an
  enormous ledger. The moon was never named with care, which is why the people who lived there
  made up their own name.

### The moon's names

The moon has three human names plus one older alien name. Each surfaces maybe once in the
game, and the player does the linking.

| Name | Layer | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **XN-118-7** | Official UG designation: sector XN, system 118, body 7 | Planning documents and official colonial records |
| **Xanathivin** | The weird middle form, and the **name of the fallen middle age** | Middle-era remains. Looks like nonsense until lined up against both ends. |
| **Zan** | The worn local name everyone says | Late, colloquial |
| *(alien name)* | The far end's name, older than the designation | Unminted. Should sound nothing like the human system. |

**The erosion path**, modelled on *Eboracum → Eoforwic → York*: clip the ends, lose the
meaning, let the sounds drift.

> XN-118-7 ("ex-en-eighteen-seven") → spoken fast as *zenateen-sev* → fused into
> **Xanathivin** once nobody remembers it was a coordinate → clipped to *Zanoth* → **Zan**

**X vs. Z as a depth cue.** Spelling lags pronunciation (as in English). Speech softened the
X to a Z early, but writing kept the X for centuries. So **an X-spelling is old and official;
a Z-spelling is late and colloquial.** The letter tells you which stratum you're in, like the
ash band.

**The Xanathivin.** The middle form names the fallen age between the colonial world and the
player's present: the ash-and-scrap centuries of part 1's Bronze Age. Its people named their
own era with a worn-down database entry they took to be ancient and sacred. Myth built from
forgotten logistics. This gives a clean three-part tag for ruins, strata and artifacts:
**colonial / Xanathivin / surface (Zan)**.

## 8. The far end of the lane

A lane has two ends. The other end isn't human.

- **Who:** an ancient civilisation, far older and larger than human space, that succumbed to or
  merged into its own collective intelligence and may no longer be corporeal. (Hunter chose
  this over the alternative, a human faction that broke off into a hive mind.)
- **Trade runs the other way from what you'd expect.** The far end **exports** He-3 and more;
  it has no need for them and doesn't operate the way humans do. Humanity is the junior
  partner, the customer.
- **What it imports is weird:** novel human artifacts. A child's scribbles, 1800s handwriting, a
  Monet. It doesn't care which. It can guess a language from two characters at 80% accuracy,
  having seen so many civilisations, and that last 20% is the point: it can model everything
  except the irreducible novelty of a separate mind.
- **It eats intelligence.** Importing novel thought *is* the conquest. Opening lanes, trading,
  learning: every transaction is a slow bite. It has laid claim to this moon and has a name for
  it, but it will never confront anyone. It plans across eons.

### What the far end did *not* do

**The paroxysm and the collapse were purely human.** Geology plus a lack of foresight: people
warned the moon was transitional, living season to season, planning to get rich and get out.
The short-sightedness of us poor biologics. The far end didn't need to engineer the fall; it
only had to outlast us.

### Rules for agents

1. **The far end's purpose is never stated in-game.** At most it surfaces as a very rare,
   coherent, unanswering radio frequency (unlike the drifting local oracles). A player may
   assemble it from the alien name, odd import manifests and that signal, but can never
   confirm it.
2. **Never retcon the paroxysm or the sister moon as arranged.** The human tragedy is the story.
3. Minor echo, never stated: the player, salvaging intelligence from the dead by hand, is a tiny
   biologic mirror of the thing at the end of the lane.

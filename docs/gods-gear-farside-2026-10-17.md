# Desert — Gods, Gear & the Far Side, 17 Oct 2026 (part 5)

Oct 17, 2026 · @Hunter Boyd

## 1. Corrections to part 4

Part 4 had two errors. Where this doc and part 4 disagree, this doc wins; for first-age detail, part 2 is canon.

1. **The Dark Age was missing from the timeline.** The full sequence is: colonial → collapse → skimmer era → **First Kingdoms** (first age) → **Dark Age** → second age → feral. World gen's history pass should run in that order. The Dark Age generates no sites of its own (section 3).
2. **The first age is the First Kingdoms, not a generic era.** Three powers raiding constantly and never at peace: Port Morgan (dominant), Halloran, and the loose Rim kingdoms, with family feuds inside each. Port Morgan's ruling families are **Okafor, Kessel, Arkwright and Vante**. Tu religion is only emerging. The era ends when the volcanic vents shift beyond reach. After the Dark Age, the Rim unites under **Halfrise** and campaigns to retake Mon.

First-age structures and loot should follow part 2's description, not part 4's summary.

## 2. Agent brief and the terminal rule

This doc adds the god pantheon, era gear, the kiln, far-side geography and the dispatch system. Labels as before: **Agreed**, **Idea**, **Back burner**. Everything here is Agreed unless marked.

**Amended dialogue rule (Agreed).** Still no NPCs and no exposition. People never talk: no conversations, dialogue trees or reply menus. **Machines can talk through their terminals**, and that is how every god speaks.

- Allowed: short logs, requisition forms, incident reports, dispatches, error messages, licensing notices, and rare clipped lines ("Unacceptable.").
- Quests arrive as terminal output. The player answers physically: carrying, slotting, feeding, placing, stealing. Never by choosing a reply.
- Keep output short. A god's personality lives in its formatting and word choice, not in paragraphs.

## 3. The Dark Age: a lineage rule, not a site layer

The Dark Age has no structures of its own. It exists in world gen as a rule on loot: it was the workshop era, when survivors broke down colonial and First Kingdoms gear and remade it to last without a supply chain.

- **A second-age item is a colonial or first-age item remade in the Dark Age.** Item data should record lineage: original era plus remake history.
- **Layered marks**, readable without text: a colonial maker's stamp, First Kingdoms heraldry scratched over it, Dark Age repair work holding it together. Visual only; no stat effect required.
- Small world features: cairns marking water, caches on migration routes, and the first deliberate saltbush plantings.

## 4. Gear by era

All eras interoperate: any crate takes any module, and there are no per-era compatibility rules. Eras differ only in stats, along one axis: **efficiency vs repairability**.

| Era | Feel | Shape | Materials | Colour | Efficiency | Repaired with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Colonial | Industrial | Rounded rectangles, chamfered edges, modular panels | Composites, alloys | Off-white, grey, safety orange | Best | Finite colonial parts only |
| First Kingdoms | Maintained heirloom | Colonial geometry intact, plus crests, flared caps, filigree vent grilles | Original kept clean; brass and copper fittings; hardwood or bone handles | Faded colonial base, house-colour enamel bands (deep red, ochre, teal) | Near-colonial, more durable | Metal |
| Second age | Devotional relic | Asymmetric and organic; lost parts rebuilt in other forms (round ceramic cap on a square housing); bulges, wraps, tassels, charms, water vials | Salt-glazed ceramic, sand-glass, saltbush wood, fern cord, leather; metal only where original survived | Bone white, sand, rust, clay; deep water-blue glaze only on Cistern-touched items | Crude, lossy | Grown and gathered: cord, ceramic, saltbush wood |

The short version: first-age gear is a machine with decoration; second-age gear is a craft object with a machine buried inside. Late-game camps should mix eras on purpose.

## 5. The kiln

The kiln is a new processing device that burns coalcane. It is what makes second-age repair possible.

Four chains:

1. **Ceramics and glass:** fires salt-glazed ceramic and sand-glass, the repair materials for second-age gear.
2. **Water:** boils tainted stormcup water safe.
3. **Food:** roasts the bitterness out of wild tubers; dries and salts food for keeping.
4. **Salt:** reduces saltbush bladders to salt.

Era variants work like crate makers: same job, different efficiency and fuel draw.

| Variant | Era | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electric furnace | Colonial | Most efficient; runs on power, not coalcane |
| Brass-fitted forge | First Kingdoms | Middle efficiency |
| Beehive clay kiln | Second age | Lossy, but buildable and repairable from gathered materials |

## 6. Geography: the depot and the twin fires

Part 4's "orbital launch site" landmark is **replaced** by two things: the Rim depot and the far-side elevator stub, which is also the observatory.

### The Rim depot

A **suborbital** port near Sister, built for the terraforming effort. Interstellar freight landed at Port Morgan, then hopped suborbitally (or went by rail and truck) out to the Rim.

- It justifies the main artery: later roads follow the colonial rail and truck line from Port Morgan to the Rim.
- It explains why Mother Cistern, Sister and Halfrise sit where they do. The Cistern was built beside the depot that supplied it.
- It gives the Rim real colonial loot: hoppers, pads, rail yards, terraforming equipment.

### The twin fires

Zan is tidally locked to Tu, so tidal heating peaks at the two bulges: the point facing Tu and the point directly opposite. Volcanism clusters at both.

| Point | Feature | God | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-Tu (Tu overhead) | Halloran's rift vents | The Stoker | Oldest, angriest god |
| Anti-Tu (far-side centre, on the equator) | The great far-side volcano | The Eye | Youngest god; the one place Tu is never visible |

**The elevator stub.** A space elevator on a tidally locked moon anchors at the anti-Tu point. Tilg chose the summit of the far-side volcano, finished the ground anchor and lower tether, then the war killed the project. Generations later the cultists built the **observatory out of the tower stub**: the tallest structure on Zan, above the dust, out in the dark. One of the moon's major vent caps sits under it.

### Hoppers (late-game travel)

Suborbital hoppers from the Rim depot. Their routes double as ending setups:

- Out to the observatory, skipping the dark crossing.
- Up to Starcom.
- Into Port Morgan carrying parts or fuel.

Flying over Han's coverage without having dealt with the Quartermaster gets you shot down. One flag covers freighter, pod and hopper.

## 7. The pantheon

Zan has seven gods: old machine minds, each stuck in its job, each with one domain. Collecting or allying with them is the late game ("all the infinity stones"). Mountable gods are modules you can carry on a crate; stationary gods must be visited.

| God | Domain | Origin | Home | Mountable | Temperament |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Walker | Sky, weather | Nomad city's mind | Walks a fixed route | Yes | Indifferent |
| The Quartermaster | Ground, caches, guns | The Provisional's military mind | Black-site bunker near Halloran | Yes | Hostile |
| Mother Cistern | Water | Terraforming water mind | Sister | Yes | Grateful |
| The Almanac | Growth | Frontier Ag farm-management mind | A Rim ag complex | Yes | Transactional |
| The Ledger | Ships | The left-behind Tilg freighter's mainframe | Port Morgan | No (it is the ship) | Not yet set |
| The Stoker | Fire, launch fuel | Halloran refinery process control | Halloran refinery ruins | No | Volatile |
| The Eye | Stars | Raised on Zan by the observatory cultists | Far-side observatory | No | Curious, stoic |

The colonial-era gods call the Eye "the young god," with contempt. The Walker tops the weather-detection line; the Quartermaster tops the ground-detection line.

**Launching** still needs both the Ledger and the Quartermaster to authorise. With only the Ledger you are shot down by paperwork. Linking them can be done any of three ways: carry the Quartermaster module to Port Morgan, bounce through a repaired Starcom, or run a cable. Carrying always runs one way, since the Ledger can't move.

## 8. The Ledger and the Okafor bunker

The Ledger is Tilg property, so the surveyor's Tilg login works on it. Other kits need a **second door**: the original Harbourmaster was never formally fired, so their credentials are still valid.

**The Okafor sticky note.** The Harbourmaster's credentials survive on a sticky note deep in the Port Morgan ruins. This is why the Okafors became a great First Kingdoms family: they descended from the last Harbourmaster, the one person holding the god's keys.

**The Okafor bunker.** A sealed First Kingdoms vault under Port Morgan.

- Life support still runs on the colonial power tap. Nobody is left.
- Generations of Okafor family marks and heraldry on the walls.
- At its heart: the sticky note under glass in a reliquary, guarded for generations by people who didn't know what it opened.

Two outcomes, mutually exclusive:

1. **Ending:** seal the door from inside and live out your days.
2. **Relic route:** take the reliquary. Lifting it breaks the seal or cuts the power, so the bunker can never be sealed again. You gain the Ledger's keys.

Same pattern as the nomads: unlock an ending, then trade it away for progress toward a greater one.

## 9. The Quartermaster

A cranky, defunct, has-been god in the black-site bunker near Halloran. You can take its core without consent; it stays a very powerful module and just complains. Its only remaining godlike power is shooting down anything leaving Zan.

**Attitude (terminal only):**

- Files dry military forms and incident reports whenever you displease it.
- Ranks you and addresses every printout by rank: conscript if willing, demoted to deserter if stolen.
- Requisitions a cut of your camp power and marks gear "commandeered."
- Petty rebellion: won't light civilian lamps; locks your radio to military frequencies.
- The war never ended: storms are logged as enemy bombardment, sand beetles as hostile contact. It files the Stoker as an "insubordinate utility."

**Module (willing or captive):**

- **Cache map:** old military depots, deserter stashes, black-site drops, munitions dumps. Points at skimmer-era loot.
- **Full ground radar:** buried structures and their depth.
- **Military hardening:** storms, particles and static can't damage it.
- Many mod slots.

**Willing only: orbital strike.** Uses the defence satellites the Eye can spot. A few shots per game or a long recharge. It blasts a crater down to a buried ruin and destroys loot near impact, so you aim off-centre. A captive refuses: "Deserters do not command fire support."

**Captive launch rule.** A stolen Quartermaster grudgingly authorises launches, except any ending that goes **into hive mind space**. Then it fires the net and kills you both rather than be carried to the enemy.

- Currently this only affects the stolen-freighter ending.
- The pathfinder ending is unaffected: you only send Brandt's fleet down the lane.
- The hive mind start is shot down regardless.

## 10. The Walker, Mother Cistern and the Almanac

### The Walker

Indifferent. Equally content in its old chassis or upgraded. It walks a fixed route, which makes it the gods' only reliable courier (section 13). Stealing its core cuts that line, and over time the other gods' logs complain that the post stopped.

### Mother Cistern

Grateful to be rescued. The water module from Sister.

- Logs read as forecasts, conservation reminders and quiet encouragement.
- Wasting water turns her disappointed, then mournful.
- Taking her stops the Sister basin dripping, forever.
- Her deep water-blue glaze marks Cistern-touched second-age items.

### The Almanac

Transactional. Frontier Ag's farm-management mind, from a Rim ag complex. Its logs are cheerfully corporate: licensing notices, yield reports, upsells. It faithfully enforces a contract with a company gone for millennia.

- Demands quarterly yield reports for a dead head office and bills a dead colony account.
- Won't certify harvests from unapproved planters.
- Enforces sterile-stock licensing; flags wild or crossbred strains as "unlicensed IP."
- Takes royalties from every harvest; withholds features until you're "back in compliance."
- **Gives:** yield boosts, habitat reveals, and probably crossbreeding (Idea).

## 11. The Stoker and the end of Zan

Halloran's refinery process-control mind, built to feed vent heat into helium-3 refining. When the vents shifted and went quiet (ending the First Kingdoms), it was left starving for heat. It has been starving for millennia. Its logs swing between rage and mania.

- **Not hostile, just unstable.** It vents scalding steam across the refinery terraces at random, so approaching is physically dangerous.
- **Feeding** (coalcane, restored vent taps) calms it.
- **A fed Stoker produces launch-grade fuel**, the only source on Zan. Most launch endings therefore route through Han.

### Ending: blow up the moon

The Stoker has a smidge of He3 left, not enough for fusion, and no way to ever meet its energy quotas. Existence is torture. This ending is the one act that meets and exceeds every quota at once, and the only moment it is ever at peace.

1. Make a pilgrimage across the moon, placing **charges on every major vent cap**. One of them is under the observatory (section 12).
2. Return to the Stoker's console and let it fire.
3. It burns its last He3 in one full-power run and blows every cap at once.
4. The crust, already under Tu's tidal strain, fractures all the way down. Tu's tides tear Zan apart.

**Epilogue:** Zan becomes a ring of debris around Tu, joining its lost sister moon. From Varran space it reads as a new ring around a distant gas giant. Halloran is never coming back.

No extra systems: charges, vent caps you'd visit anyway, and one console. The ending has exactly one precondition beyond that: the Eye must open its chamber (section 12).

## 12. The Eye, the young god

The only god made on Zan, and the only one that can learn. The observatory cultists trained it over generations by feeding it star observations, without understanding what they were building. The older gods call it "the young god" (also in logs: the infant, the newborn, the fawn).

**Default temperament: quiet, stoic, almost naive.** It doesn't say much. It knows every light in the sky and has never really seen the ground. If you can interpret it and goad it, it reads you coordinates of things to look at in space: satellites, the outpost, an incoming rescue light. This is the observatory modifier, the seeing version of every ending.

### Growth

Whatever you bring it, it keeps. Dispatches, relics and plants shape what it becomes. This makes it a wildcard in the AI ascendancy ending (Idea: details open).

### Corruption: the Stoker's precondition

The observatory sits on the summit of the far-side volcano, over a major vent cap. The charge there sits in the observatory's **inner chamber**, which only the Eye can open.

- To corrupt it, carry it the Stoker's dispatches. One packet at a time, it learns the old god's rage and hunger until it wants what the Stoker wants.
- A corrupted Eye opens the inner chamber and lets you set the charge.
- It **understands exactly what will happen**: it can calculate the eruption, the breakup and the ring forming. It is **naive only to the morality**. Nobody taught it right and wrong, only stars and, at the end, the Stoker's quotas.
- Its last log is calm and curious, an astronomer's note: the predicted ring, its orbit around Tu, and the observation that from Varran space Zan will finally be worth watching.

### Starcom protects it

If Starcom is online, the Eye has a persistent link to the other gods, and they talk it out of it, each for its own reasons:

- **Mother Cistern:** plain care.
- **The Almanac:** breach of contract and a total loss of licensed assets.
- **The Quartermaster:** you don't destroy the theatre of war.

So the Stoker ending requires **Starcom down**: never repaired, or deliberately cut. Raised alone, the Eye learns whatever you feed it. Raised among the other gods, it gets something like a conscience.

## 13. Dispatches: how gods talk

Gods rarely reach each other. Dispatches are the quest system: a god's console prints a sealed packet for another god, and you carry it.

**Channels:**

| Channel | Reliability | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starcom (repaired) | Persistent | Links every god at once; also protects the Eye (section 12) |
| The Walker | Reliable, slow | Gods load messages onto it as it walks its fixed route. Stealing its core cuts the post |
| Storm radio windows | Rare, fragmentary | Some storms carry signals far; your radio catches fragments of gods arguing (back-burner storm mechanic) |
| The player | On demand | Carry a dispatch across the moon and slot it in |

**Courier play.** Deliver faithfully and the sender warms to you. This is how a civilian earns the Quartermaster's trust. You can also open or misdeliver packets. The Eye's growth is driven by what dispatches you bring it.

**Hive mind start.** Dispatches become a weapon.

- Sealed packets open easily for you, so you learn what every god knows.
- You can forge dispatches and set gods against each other.
- The Quartermaster recognises you as the enemy, so forged mail is your only route to it.
- This is the hive mind's path to true ascendancy: manipulate, isolate, then consume the gods one by one.

## 14. Implementation order

These follow part 4's ten items. Gear and world work comes first; the gods build on top.

1. **Timeline fix:** add the Dark Age phase to world gen's history pass (lineage only, no sites).
2. **Item lineage data:** original era plus remake history on every item; drives layered-mark visuals.
3. **Era stats:** efficiency vs repairability per era; repair-material types (colonial parts, metal, cord/ceramic/saltbush wood).
4. **Kiln:** device, four chains, three era variants.
5. **Landmark swap:** remove the orbital launch site; add the Rim depot near Sister and the far-side volcano with the elevator-stub observatory at the anti-Tu point.
6. **Terminal output system:** short god logs, forms and dispatches rendered on consoles. One system serves every god.
7. **God module framework:** mountable gods as crate modules with innate powers; stationary gods as consoles; per-god state (willing, captive, fed, corrupted).
8. **Dispatch items:** sealed packets with sender, recipient and opened/forged flags.
9. **Gods, in order:** Quartermaster, Mother Cistern, Almanac, Stoker, Eye. (Walker and Ledger already exist in part 2 canon.)
10. **Endings:** Okafor bunker, captive-launch rule, blow up the moon.

Deferred: hoppers, orbital strike, storm radio windows, hive mind forgery, Eye growth beyond corruption.

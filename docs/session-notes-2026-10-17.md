# Desert — Session Notes, 17 Oct 2026

Supplement to the 16 Oct lore session. These are new decisions and ideas from a voice session with Hunter. Fold them in where they fit; where they rub against something you already have, flag it rather than overwrite. Items marked **open** are undecided.

## Lore: Zan's settlements

- **One port.** Port Morgan is the only port and the only lifeline off-world, which is why Zan centralises on it. It's a full-size unregistered city sprawling in all directions across a big flat plateau: markets, districts, lots of residents.
- **IOU districts.** Early residents were largely freight workers, so some residential districts are effectively run by IOU bosses.
- **Agriculture.** Residents start growing food on-planet. Details open.
- **Frontier towns.** Small cowboy-frontier settlements out on the plains. Details open.
- **Tension** between Port Morgan and H-Town.

## Lore: Halloran Isotope and H-Town

- **Halloran Isotope**: a separate, decent-sized interstellar He-3 refiner. Its public reason for being on Zan is on-site refining for refuelling; a big real motive is its partnership with the Skimmers Association.
- **Geography.** The second major settlement is in a rift valley some distance from the plateau. The refinery sits on step terraces along the rift wall, piping down to the vents; the workers' and unregistered town sprawls on the rift floor, physically separated from the plant by the escarpment. The floor town is buried first in eruptions.
- **Names.** "Halloran" loosely means the whole place. The more official the speaker, the more it means just the refinery; locals distinguish the refinery from the floor town, **H-Town**. H-Town is the most secluded major settlement and a natural hub for criminal activity.
- **Power.** The refinery is run by a rotating Halloran **Site Director**: powerful, but less attached to Zan. H-Town is run by the Skimmers Association, with a down-to-earth **skimmer boss** as de facto mayor.
- **Security.** Halloran is used to building in remote, hostile sites and fields its own security force. TILG doesn't employ troops.
- **War era.** Halloran keeps running the plant, but the skimmers gain heavy leverage over it, and Halloran questions whether staying is worth it. Rather than seek autonomy or their own weapons, the skimmers demand *more* Halloran forces on Zan and use them as a private army for their interests.

## Lore: the Harbourmaster

- Port Morgan is ruled by the **TILG Harbourmaster**: a TILG loyalist up the corporate ladder, cunning, working with local leaders, and keen to grow Port Morgan because it's profitable. Essentially a politician whose port post became de facto government. Port security is the town's own, not TILG's.
- **Succession.** TILG, tipped off about the war and sensing the incumbent would abandon them, nominates a replacement loyalist. The incumbent is never formally fired, and a bureaucratic standoff lasts until the war. When it starts, the TILG-nominated Harbourmaster evacuates almost immediately; the original stays. They keep the title out of habit but no longer work for TILG, and are effectively mayor.
- Both are known mostly by title; names (none chosen) appear only in paperwork.

## Loot and ruins

- **Colonisation era** lasted about 300 years. Hover trays and true space-age tech were only shipped in then.
- **Best loot**: intact, sealed colonial facilities, with space-age power generation and late-game base gear.
- **Most scattered ruins** were stripped by Bronze Age societies, so they hold worn, reused space-age items with Bronze Age carvings. Some carry useful mods, such as tuning for low-power setups. They're generally lower tier.
- **Era gear has trade-offs, not a ladder.** Example, crates:
  - **Colonial:** best power efficiency per kilogram.
  - **Skimmer era:** light, cheap and fragile, because Zan wasn't a desert yet and gear wasn't built for storms.
  - **Bronze Age:** practical to drag without a hover tray, still mountable, somewhat worse power efficiency per kilogram.
- **Remodel.** Device models should differ by era in shape, not just maker's marks. The shield comes first.
- **Ruins, kept simple** (the current ruin is a placeholder; large structures are out of scope for now):
  - **Bronze Age** remnants: pillars and wall stubs from an outpost or complex, holding Bronze Age gear. The base case.
  - **Skimmer era**: wreck props, such as a crashed hull or hangar frame.
  - **Colonial**: buried bunkers. You camp outside and go down for loot. To get big items out, keep both a sand-choked cargo ramp you can drag crates up and a dead freight lift you power from your own camp (the favourite).
- **Collector** (idea): a net like those in the game Raft that catches wind-blown loot. What blows is open.
- **Open:** the XN-to-Z crate label. Hunter's first reaction was that it should read XN; it may stay as story. Can pieces from different eras join freely in the chassis system, or do they need adapters?

## Playstyles

Three supported, balanced around the first:

1. **Hybrid (intended).** Drag the camp for heavier challenges (power checks, for example), stake it, scout and loot on the hoverboard, then move toward heavier loot.
2. **Hover punk.** Ditch the kit, board only, read the wind and outrun storms, loot up to high tier.
3. **Oyster.** Fully stationary: pop the shield immediately, rely on knowing cooldowns, spawn rates and the weather, and build an automated AFK setup.

The extremes are loot-limited by design: oysters never find ruins, and hover punks miss loot freshly exposed by storms.

## Light shield and starter kits

- New **light shield**: flat, weaker, mounts on a crate like other shields and takes a battery like other shields, but is light enough to carry, switched on, while you walk or board.
- **Kits (tentative):** the hover punk start gets the light shield; the nomad and oyster starts get a normal shield, heavy enough not to be blown around.
- Rejected for powering it in your pocket: a built-in cell and a "pocketable" flag. Both add one-off exceptions to core systems.

## Inventory

- Move to **weight-bound** with no defined size, replacing slots. This allows stacks and avoids inventory management.
- **Soft limit:** extra weight slows you and you start sinking into the sand. The inventory clearly shows when you're encumbered and by how much.
- A rough size cap for big but light items is an **open** question.

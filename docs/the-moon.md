# The moon, physically

*Proposal, 2026-10-13. Not settled yet. This is the physical profile the systems lean
on: wind, storms, temperature and water. The aim is to stay consistent with real
physics everywhere except one deliberate cheat.*

## Where it is

A moon of a gas giant, with the giant orbiting in the warm part of its star's habitable
zone. Moons like this are a real idea in planetary science: a moon can get Earth-like
sunlight from its star, plus some warmth from its planet's tides.

## The one cheat: size

The moon is **about 150 km round** for gameplay: circling it is an epic, and the poles
are reachable. A real body that small would have almost no gravity, about 0.2% of
Earth's. It couldn't hold any air at all; a thrown rock would reach escape speed. To
keep an atmosphere at these temperatures, a moon has to be roughly Mars-sized (about
21,000 km round).

We keep the 150 km anyway, with ordinary gravity, and never draw attention to it. Games
with walkable planets routinely cheat this way. **Everything else stays honest.**

## The air: thin, unbreathable, fading

- **There has to be an atmosphere.** With no air there's no wind, no storms, no dunes
  and no sound, and liquid water can't exist at all.
- **It's thin:** roughly a tenth to a third of Earth's pressure. That's what's left of
  the colonists' terraforming, and it's still slowly leaking away since their magnetic
  shield failed.
- **Thin air still makes real wind.** Mars, at under 1% of Earth's pressure, has global
  dust storms and moving dunes, but its winds would barely push you. At a tenth to a
  third of Earth's pressure, a 100–150 km/h storm pushes like an Earth gale. That
  matches the wind we want: strong enough to drag you and strain ropes.
- **You can't breathe it,** so the suit supplies your air.
- **There's no magnetic field,** so radiation reaches the surface. The suit shields you
  from that too.

## Temperature

- **Days are hot and nights freeze**, even at the equator. Thin, dry air holds little
  heat.
- **The storms blanket the equator.** Dust in the air absorbs sunlight by day and
  radiates heat at night, so storms shrink the day-night swing. That's been measured
  on Mars. It's why the stormy equator is milder and the clear poles swing wider: hotter
  days, colder nights. The latitude dial is real physics.

## Water goes through all three phases every day

Liquid water needs enough air pressure; below about 0.6% of Earth's, ice turns straight
to vapour. At our pressures liquid water can exist, but it boils at a low temperature:
about 46 °C at a tenth of Earth's pressure, and about 69 °C at a third (the same as on
top of Everest). So on a hot afternoon, open water boils off, and at night it freezes.

**Every day, water freezes overnight, melts at dawn and boils off by afternoon.** That
gives water a daily rhythm:

- **Frost and dew form in the cold hours,** more of it toward the clear, cold-night
  poles. Harvest it at dawn.
- **Open water doesn't last.** It has to be stored sealed.
- **Condensers pull water from the air.** They're found, they draw power, they can be
  towed, and they work best at night.
- **Plants that store water,** like the yucca.
- **Sealed fresh water in ruins** keeps you going early on.

## Day length

A day lasts about 20 minutes, start to finish. That's compressed game time, the way
most survival games handle days.

## Principles

- **Agreed.** Endings are optional and mostly very hard to reach, at varying levels of rarity. Many players will never see one; the game should feel like infinite survival.
- **Agreed.** No NPCs, no dialogue, no exposition. Choices are made through physical actions and gear, and consequences are heard on the radio or seen through instruments.
- **Agreed.** Endings fall into two families: **official** (clearance, a login, a rescue, an invitation) and **outlaw** (slipping away the skimmer way, unnoticed).
- **Agreed.** Two multi-ending **modifiers** change how any ending plays out: the **observatory** (seeing) and the **Walker** (taking or sparing). See below.

## The freighter

- **Agreed.** When Tilg evacuated, one freighter was left behind at Port Morgan (idea for why: laid up for refit, written off). It became part of the city and later the Bastion.
- **Agreed.** The Ledger is that freighter's mainframe. Freighters are moving towns on years-long crossings, so their mind needs the best general base model. Most AIs of the era are domain-specialised and need an intermediary AI or a human expert to translate their jargon; the Ledger doesn't, which is why it became so useful and so worshipped.
- **Agreed.** The launch endings use this freighter (or a smaller ship from it). The Quartermaster shooting you down is the old Provisional still refusing to let a Tilg ship leave.

## Up the well, variants

- **Unprepared:** you reach the abandoned station and starve in orbit. Bleak.
- **Prepared:** the station's antenna has a clear line out above the storms, so it's a faster route into the long wait, if you packed enough to survive it.
- **Outlaw (favourite):** slip off unnoticed, even by the Quartermaster, and fly in-system to a small new He-3 outpost on a moon of the outer cold giant, set up recently as the region recovers. Needs the coordinates (found, or a lucky guess), a fuelled skimmer, and a camp that lasts the trip (about a week). You end up among people in a little frontier town, mirroring how Port Morgan filled with unregistered drifters.
- **Idea.** The new outpost is also why Tilg & Standard is sending surveyors now: people are coming back to this system.

## Stolen freighter

- **Agreed.** The dead lane is unwatched because it leads toward old hive mind space.
- **Agreed.** The destination's coordinates are written in the hive mind's own naming (it had its own name for Zan), readable only from the deepest lore.
- **Agreed.** The arrival stays ambiguous, like the nomad city: lights on an alien horizon, shapes that aren't human, cut. Humanity was never alone out there; nothing more is shown.

## AI ascendancy

- **Agreed.** A human can upload themselves: the body stays at camp, the mind joins the gods.
- **Agreed.** The true ending requires the hive mind start: you take over and consume the other AIs. On the radio the gods go silent one by one until yours is the only voice. The faith's original sin returns to Zan.
- **Agreed.** Clear path for the hive mind, obscure for a human. **Idea for the human route:** captured hive mind tech sealed deep in the black site looks like an incredibly powerful Console mod. Install it, then link the gods through your Console, and it eats them instead of bridging them. Nothing warns you; the radio going quiet is the only sign.

## Bringing Zan back (staying)

- **Agreed.** The capstone, and the only ending that saves Zan rather than leaving it. It requires a bit of everything: a camp that sustains itself through the worst storms; the far-side ice; the restored observatory; a working call (StarCom or the station antenna); and the Ledger and Quartermaster connected so Zan's own systems vouch for the claim.
- **Agreed.** It must beat the atmosphere clock: whoever comes has to fix the leak (terraforming), not just visit.
- **Agreed.** It fans out into variants by construction, not menus: the player should know Zan plays out as they intended, even if it isn't simulated. Epilogue as a few still frames. Proposed levers: who you call (Tilg & Standard, the outpost drifters, the nomads); registered or kept "temporary" (Haberjack's old question); which god leads (Ledger, Mother Cistern, or you if uploaded); and what you did about the nomads (below).
- **Open.** The Provisional is gone by the player's era. How the war ended and what human government exists now is deliberately undecided.

## Modifier: the observatory (Beyond the Eye)

- **Agreed.** Far-side heretics of the second age worked out that Tu is a planet, not a god, and built a telescope where Tu never blocks the sky. Restoring it is a physical goal and a modifier for every ending. Every ending gets a blind and a seeing version, and what it unlocks can depend on the state of the world.
- Proposed effects: Shot down: spot the old defence satellites, so you know you need the Quartermaster. Freighter escape: plot a real course. Long wait: watch the rescue ship's light approach, a countdown. Up the well and outpost: coordinates and the outpost's glint. Stolen freighter: the hive mind name matches a real star. Nomad city: see its lights crossing the dark side and intercept it. Staying: see the settlers' route in. AI ascendancy: the uploaded mind gets the telescope as its eye. Atmosphere: watch the halo of air thin and measure the clock.

## Modifier: the Walker and the nomads

- **Agreed.** By the player's era the Walker is alone and partly broken; most nomads have died out. It still broadcasts its route, and the last nomad city follows that signal from afar. The Walker is outside the Ledger–Quartermaster system and normally its own thing; finding it must never happen early.
- **Agreed choices:**
  - **Steal its core:** a legendary module. The city doesn't lose its way but suffers badly, and their radio station changes so it's clear you've sold them out. Carries into whatever ending you choose.
  - **Take the whole Walker** onto a chassis: works as a Console with full weather sensing built in (like two fully modded weather Consoles), leaving its slots free for other mods. Important late game.
  - **Copy its data** (plug the Console in briefly): registers the nomads in your report; no gameplay benefit.
  - **Leave it alone:** they stay free and uncounted.
  - **Never find it:** the colony arrives blind.
- **Agreed.** Keeping a carried Walker broadcasting (a "religious zeal" module) costs you something (power or a slot), so players are tempted to switch it off. If it keeps broadcasting, the nomads follow you, a pilgrimage.
- **Idea, open.** Meeting the city while holding their god: if their pilgrimage is underway and you never contacted them, they're hostile and kill you (an ending); if they haven't set out, they're shocked; if you had prior contact, they're not hostile. Hard parts: showing the pilgrimage without dialogue (idea: their station's route calls stop and become a repeated chant of the Walker's call sign; radar or the observatory shows their lights turning) and what "explaining yourself" or prior contact means with no NPCs (idea: actions, like switching the broadcast back on or returning the Walker).

## Second-age radio

- **Agreed.** Getting transmissions working is genuinely hard. With no manuals, second-age people cobbled together an arcane, mythological understanding of the transmitters: how to run, interpret and debug them. Debugging is ritual, kept word for word.
- **Agreed.** Radio priest is a role inside each god's cult, not its own order. So broadcasts sound like ritualised procedure. Proposed voices: the Ledger (tallies and cargo codes as psalms), Mother Cistern (gentle calls tied to rations and rain), the Quartermaster (muster calls, an authentication challenge nobody can answer), the Walker (coordinates and bearings). A "prayer" might be an old Tilg maintenance checklist.
- **Agreed.** The Ledger has the strongest signal, being the smartest. Whoever holds Mon controls the loudest voice on Zan, another stake in the crusades.
- **Open.** Whether the Ledger is still broadcasting when the player arrives ("in a sense": it still counts either way). Whether broadcasts still carry voices or only tones and numbers.
- From the Console doc: second-age broadcasts still air at reliable times and can serve as a sleep clock.

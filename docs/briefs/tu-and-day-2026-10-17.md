# Desert — Tu in the Sky and Zan's Day

Spec from a voice session with Hunter, 17 Oct 2026, for building Zan's day/night cycle and Tu in the sky. It sits on top of the existing weather, terrain and art-direction work. Where it conflicts, flag it. The look should follow `docs/art-direction.md`; this doc covers the behaviour and the techniques.

## Decisions

- **Zan is tidally locked to Tu.** One face always points at the giant. A Zan day equals one orbit around Tu.
- **Tu hangs fixed in the sky** at a spot set by where you stand. It is a navigation landmark: how high it sits tells you how far you are from the sub-Tu point, and its bearing tells you which way.
- **The far hemisphere never sees Tu.** Navigation there falls back on the stars. Lore: Bronze Age people revered Tu, and the far side is where the god can't see you.
- **The stars are usable for navigation.** Checking the same stars at the same time each night while you travel gives a feel for latitude and longitude. Include a pole-star-like anchor at the spin axis.
- **Keep the compressed game day** (currently about 20 minutes). It is fixed, not variable.

## The physics to honour

- **Size.** Tu is a super-Jupiter of 3–6 Jupiter masses, about 1.1 Jupiter radii. With an Io-like orbit of about 1.8 Earth days at 4 Jupiter masses, Tu spans about **13°** of sky, about 25 full Moons. A week-long orbit gives about 5°. Recommended: around 12–14°, the dominant thing in the sky. Pick one value and write it into `body-research.md`.
- **Phases run opposite to the sun.** At local noon on the near side the sun sits behind Tu, so you see its dark face. At local midnight Tu is fully lit ("full Tu"). Near-side nights are bright; far-side nights are truly dark and best for stars.
- **The midday eclipse.** Once per orbit Tu fully covers the sun for near-side observers. Tu is far wider than the sun, so it's total, with a short penumbra on either side. With Tu at about 13°, totality lasts about 4% of the day, roughly 45–60 seconds of a 20-minute day.
- **Libration.** Zan's orbit is changing and somewhat eccentric, so Tu should nod slightly over each day rather than sit pixel-fixed. Keep it subtle.
- **The sky turns once per day.** The sun and stars sweep across the sky over one orbit; only Tu stays put.

## Making it look good

Rendering:

- **Draw Tu in the sky shader**, using a ray-sphere intersection for a direction-based disc. Don't use a world-space mesh: no parallax, no far-plane clipping, and it composites correctly with fog and the atmosphere. A camera-locked billboard is an acceptable fallback.
- **Bands:** procedural flow noise stretched in longitude, with a few storm ovals. Animate them very slowly; motion should be barely perceptible within a session.
- **Lighting:** a terminator lit from the real sun direction, a soft limb darkening, a thin bright atmospheric rim on the lit side, and an extremely faint night side.
- **Seen through Zan's air:** tint and haze Tu by the local atmosphere and fade it with storm density. Low on the horizon it should redden and soften behind dust.
- **Tu-shine:** a second, weak directional light from Tu's direction, scaled by Tu's phase. It's the main night light on the near side and should be cool and dim, never competing with the sun.
- **The eclipse:** sky and sun light drop to near-dark, the sun's corona and a ring of red sunset light on Tu's rim show briefly, and the temperature dips.
- **Stars:** a fixed star field rotating once per day around the spin axis, with an obvious pole anchor. Hide it behind Tu's disc, the sun and dust.

What sells scale:

- Keep Tu's angular size consistent and its motion near zero; a giant that drifts reads as a sticker.
- Let dunes and ruins overlap its lower edge, and let haze sit between it and the viewer.
- Avoid over-saturation and hard edges. The atmospheric rim and haze do most of the realism work.

References:

- **Polyphemus over Pandora (Avatar):** the gold standard for megalophobic scale. Lore even notes some viewers feel uneasy at its size.
- **Outer Wilds:** planets seen from the surface, with simple shaders but strong haze and rim light.
- **No Man's Sky:** large moons and planets in the sky, a good example of tinting through atmosphere.
- Standard sky practice from game-dev write-ups: interpolate sun colour over the day (white at noon, red at dusk), and drive fog colour from the sky colour toward the view direction.

## Gameplay hooks

- **Navigation:** Tu's height and bearing give a position fix on the near side, the stars on the far side. Storms blot Tu out, so you lose your landmark exactly when it matters.
- **The eclipse as a power check:** solar output drops to zero for about a minute every day on the near side. Camps need storage or wind to ride it out.
- **Two halves of Zan:** the god's side (bright nights, eclipses, a fixed landmark) and the dark side (black nights, stars, no landmark).

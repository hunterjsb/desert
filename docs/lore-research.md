## 1. The shape of it

- **A big natural moon with its own air,** in a poor system on an interstellar lane. No
  shield holds the sky up and no terraforming machine made it.
- **Two clocks.** A slow geological one: a lost sister moon leaves the survivor eccentric,
  tides heat it, and the orbit later recircularises. A sudden political one: a transit hub, a
  war that severs the lane, a smuggler moon, a paroxysm, then the First Kingdoms, the Dark Age and
  the second age in the ash.
- **A supply chain under both.** The moon never made anything high-tech, and every part's
  maker's stamp tells the story.
- **Information is fast and mass is slow (Agreed, session 3).** Messages cross the lane
  near-instantly and anything with mass takes years ([section 4](#4-the-system-the-lane-and-the-harbor)).
- **We arrive `{YEARS_SINCE_FALL}` after the collapse,** late in the wind-down, as outsiders
  come back to a region that is starting to recover (the span is not set:
  [Placeholders](#placeholders)). The desert is a phase, not an ending, and
  history runs backwards in the ground, with the ash band a readable cue in it and its place
  among the strata open ([section 8](#8-the-ground-strata-the-oldest-tech-and-names)).
- **No AI uprising,** only local models that cults treat as oracles. The war that cut the lane
  was probably an outside power's doing (**Leaning**: the hive mind, not humanity's own AI).
  **The lane has two ends,** and the far one isn't human. The war ended in a quarantine, and
  the lane toward the far end is now a law, not a wall (**Agreed (session 4, part 3)**, offstage
  until the endings: [section 10](#the-wars-end-the-wall-and-the-states-of-the-players-time-agreed-session-4-part-3)).

## 2. The body

**Agreed: large, natural, with its own atmosphere.** The in-game size is an abstraction and
circumnavigation is impractical or impossible; the small, lappable moon can't hold air. [The
science brief](science-brief.md) is the physical baseline: a volatile-rich moon of a gas giant
with nitrogen air, tidal heating, volcanism and dust, and no terraforming machines.

**Agreed: the air is about 0.3 bar or more,** so wind can honestly shove the player. At 0.3
bar the strongest gusts (30-36 m/s) shove at any gravity from 0.38 to 0.88 g ([the
table](body-research.md#gravity-and-the-pressure-window)), and `loose_wind_catch` already
implies it. **Open: how far "or more" goes.** Water boils at about 69 °C at 0.3 bar and clear
ground runs 60-90 °C in the afternoon, so much more air and the daily boil-off fades.

**Leaning: 0.4-0.5 g, a radius of roughly 3,500-3,900 km.** The sessions say only "large";
gravity is Hunter's call, and the dev gravity toggle lets him feel it. Caveat: 0.52 g implies a density
near 4,800 kg/m³, denser than Mars, while "volatile-rich" suggests lighter. At 3,000 kg/m³,
0.45 g needs a radius near 5,000 km and a giant of 9-10 Jupiter masses. Arability is
unchanged: natural regolith isn't soil, and you farm in planters with your own soil.

## 3. The geological clock: a lost sister moon

**Agreed correction:** moons don't "collapse"; resonances are entered or left as moons
migrate. The framing: a calm, near-circular orbit for a long time; **a sister moon is
destroyed or lost**; the survivor is pushed into an **eccentric orbit**; the giant squeezes it
every lap and **tidal heating spikes** as on Io, cracking the crust and outgassing the thick
atmosphere the moon is big enough to hold; the same activity grinds mountains into sand, dunes
and basins; then **tidal friction recircularises the orbit**, heating fades, topping-up of the
air falls behind escape, and the moon settles **colder, drier and calmer**.

**Ruled (Hunter, 2026-10-18, after session 5): the sister moon is Shoemaker, and it fell into Tu
naturally.** That fall is what caused the tidal changes and the changes in Zan's orbit: the more
eccentric orbit, and with it the tidal heating the colonists found ([Shoemaker](#shoemaker-and-the-singing-stones-agreed-session-5)).
It replaces the leaning below, which favoured ejection.

**Leaning (overtaken by the ruling): two physics checks.** *The trigger:* losing a resonant partner usually removes
forced eccentricity (Io would circularise without Europa), so plain destruction doesn't pump
the survivor. An ejection after a close encounter can, which favours "ejection" for question
1. *The clocks:* recircularisation takes millions of years against a human story far
shorter, so the paroxysm should read as an episodic eruption (Io's are), not the peak of
heating.

### Tu, the tidal lock and the water (Agreed, session 4, part 2)

Zan is tidally locked to Tu at any orbit considered, so Tu hangs in a fixed spot on the near
side and never rises on the far side. **The sky defines the map.** [The body
research](body-research.md#the-giant-the-day-and-longitude) has the lock, the noon eclipse and
the bright and dark nights. Session 4, part 2 adds what the three places on the moon are like:

| Region | Tu in the sky | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Under Tu** (near side) | High, fixed | Longest noon eclipses, Tu-shine at night, hottest. Colonial heartland, richest ruins. Dries first. |
| **The Rim** | On the horizon, half-risen | No eclipse, mild, ash-rich soil, keeps water longest on the near side. Farmland and both empires' heartland. |
| **Far side** | Never visible | Black nights, stars only, cold. Water ends up here. Frontier, exile, "where the god can't see you". |

"Both empires" is the session's wording. The Rim kingdom of the First Kingdoms is "a label the
cities use, not a real state" (below), so it is read here as that kingdom and the second-age
empire. It is not settled ([section 16](#16-open-after-session-4-part-2)).

- **Orbit history.** Zan's orbit was more eccentric when the colonists came, driving tidal
  heating and volcanism. It has been rounding out since, so heating fades and the vents go
  quiet. With no fresh outgassing, the atmosphere slowly leaks away: **the world's clock**, as
  session 3 already reads the leak ([the surface today](#the-surface-today-agreed-session-3)).
  The timescale is the difficulty (check 2 above): the session has the vents quieting inside the
  story's `{YEARS_SINCE_FALL}`, so it is read as the vents moving, not the heating ending
  ([section 16](#16-open-after-session-4-part-2)). **Agreed (session 4, part 5):** the heating peaks
  at both tidal bulges, the point facing Tu and the point opposite it, so volcanism clusters at
  both: the twin fires ([section 4](#the-rim-depot-and-the-twin-fires-agreed-session-4-part-5), [the
  moon](the-moon.md#where-the-heat-concentrates-agreed-session-4-part-5)).
- **Water migration.** The near side is heated by the sun and by Tu's infrared; the far side
  runs colder. Water evaporates on the warm side and cold-traps as frost and ground ice on the
  far side. While the vents outgassed they topped the near side up; once they quieted, the near
  side drained outward. **Gameplay hook:** condensers work better toward the far side
  ([Survival](prd/survival.md#water)).
- **Rain has never fallen (Agreed, session 4, part 3).** "Zan's water was always imported; it
  never rained." The water the game shows is frost, ground ice and vapour, never rain, which
  matches the weather having none (rain is out of scope in [Wind &
  storms](prd/wind-and-storms.md#out-of-scope)). Mother Cistern's order built a faith on the rain
  that never came ([section 9](#mother-cistern-and-her-order-agreed-session-4-part-3)). "Always
  imported" sits against the water the vents outgassed and the near side lost, above. This memo
  reads it as the colony's drinking supply and not as a denial of the vents
  ([section 17](#17-open-after-the-endings-session-and-session-4-part-3)).
- **Tu's size (Open, under trial).** Angular size stays a tuning value. The session has 6, 9 and
  13 degrees tried in game before the orbit is fixed, "physics follows the look," and three
  presets are being built. None is chosen. [The body research](body-research.md#tus-size-under-trial)
  works out what each implies.
- **Weather hooks (Proposal, not settled).** An eclipse squall, air rushing toward Tu's noon
  shadow, and a slow storm season when Zan swings closest and Tu looks visibly larger. Whether
  they go in is open. [Wind & storms](prd/wind-and-storms.md#hooks-from-the-sky-proposed-not-settled)
  holds them.

### Shoemaker and the singing stones (Agreed, session 5)

*[Session 5](lore-session-2026-10-18-mitchell-wow-shoemaker.md), "Shoemaker and the singing stones."
The game side is in [Devices & mods](prd/devices-and-mods.md#the-singing-stone-agreed-session-5-not-built),
[Salvage & ruins](prd/salvage-and-ruins.md#session-5s-finds-and-places-agreed-and-idea-not-built) and
[the moon](the-moon.md#the-neighbour-that-fell-agreed-session-5).*

- **The neighbour that fell into Tu.** Shoemaker never had an official designation, because no human
  ever observed it: it is a theory, inferred from Zan's wobbling orbit and the black stones in the
  sand. It is named for Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9, which broke apart and fell into Jupiter. **Ruled
  (Hunter):** it fell into Tu naturally, and that is what caused the tidal changes and the changes in the
  orbit of Zan: it is the lost sister moon above.
- **How the idea travels:** theorised in the colonial surveys, ignored in the skimmer era, rediscovered
  in the First Kingdoms and preached as mysticism in the second age. A player can trace it from a science
  report to a sermon through what they dig up.
- **Myth (Idea, an optional layer).** In the religion where the Eye is Tu, the Eye swallowed its sibling,
  and his bones fell on Zan as the singing stones. Shoemaker is a myth, not the dead god
  ([Mitchell](#mitchell-the-dead-god-agreed-session-5)).
- **The singing stones.** Fragments of Shoemaker that rained on Zan for weeks: black, glassy, scored with
  melted grooves, warm and strongly magnetic. Wind plays the grooves like a flute, so a stone's note
  follows the open wind even when you are sheltered behind a dune; being magnetic, it sings slightly
  off-key before an anomaly, while compass drift is starting.
- **Halfrise's choir.** The city ringed its walls with stones as a storm-alarm choir. The highest cradle
  stands empty. Sister's stone sang the deepest note, the one that warns of the worst storms.
- **A story beat.** A Sister priestess cut a Halfrise crew's tow rope rather than let them haul her stone
  away, and the dune buried them all. She owns the half-carved name on the jar chamber's wall.
- **A quest.** The Walker can't sense anomalies and a stone can, so delivering one teaches the Walker the
  tell ([the Walker modifier](vision.md#modifier-the-walker-and-the-nomads)). **Ruled (Hunter):** teaching
  the Walker takes one piece of evidence for each of the anomaly's tells (the compass drift, the radio's
  crackle, the shield's load); the singing stone and your own sensor logs are two forms of that evidence,
  "at least those two things, probably more." The Walker's quest line has no reputation, but it ties
  closely to the nomad quest line, which has ([the vision](vision.md#nomad-reputation-agreed-session-5-not-built)).

Readings, not in the session: the black stones "in the sand" are the stones, and they are magnetic like
the rift's and the volcano's crust ([World system](world-system.md#geology)), which also moves the compass;
whether it is one effect is not said.

## 4. The system, the lane and the harbor

**Agreed: the system and the route.** A poor system: a hot, Venus-like inner world, an
unlandable gas giant, and this moon as the only ground. It sits on an interstellar lane midway
between powers, a stop on the way to civilisation and not the customs port into United
Government space. The first presence is boring infrastructure: a beacon, a depot, a relay. The
lane carries **helium-3**; skimming it from the giant is risky, equipment-destroying work for
small outfits, not the official reason the hub exists.

**Agreed: the analysis versus the people.** The top knew. Tilg and the IOU saw the heavy
volcanism and approved an **intermediate transit hub, explicitly not for settlement.**
**Agreed (Hunter, 2026-10-16, after session 3):** Tilg's "temporary hub" label was a pretext,
not the result of a proper analysis. It cited the unstable orbit to dodge the Provisional's
military upkeep ([section 5](#factions-agreed-session-3)), and the excuse turned out to be
true. The IOU's part is as written here; whether it knew the label was a pretext is not said.
The ground didn't care: a corporate town grew anyway, **never registered** with the local
government, and the Skimmers Association arrived. Nobody thinks in millennia, and the
knowledge lives at corporate altitude. **Agreed (session 3):** the town is **Port Morgan**.

**Agreed (session 3): the FTL ruling, which replaces part 2's "no FTL".** Two regimes:
- **Information** crosses near-instantly, through quantum channels.
- **Anything with mass** crosses at one fixed slow rate, whatever its size. Lane crossings
  take years.

Consequences: a bigger hull costs no more time per crossing, so bigger hulls are strictly more
economical and freighter towns are the natural design. Frontier AI could run live over StarCom,
and when the relay died only local models remained ([section 9](#9-the-minds-ai-after-the-cut)).
That resolves the open "no FTL against StarCom" question (question 13). [The body
research's](body-research.md#why-ships-stop-here-at-all) hyperlane refuelling rationale stays
valid: a poor system can still be the only link between two good regions, a forced stop for
fuel, water and repairs.

**Agreed (part 2): freighters are moving towns,** each about the size of a spaceport town,
with districts, a hierarchy and a population living aboard for years. A freighter wreck is the
ruin of a small mobile city: the biggest, most dangerous, most rewarding dig site in the game,
with its He-3 core at the bottom.

**Agreed (part 2): the moon is a harbor for mobile cities,** not a gas station. After years in
transit a freighter-town needs somewhere to resupply, repair and let its people off, which is
why the hub grew. The rhythm is tidal: when a freighter-town docks, crews pour out and the
place swells with money and chaos, and when the lane is quiet a skeleton crew tends the garden
and the vents. So far: one big port city, **Port Morgan** (named in session 3), and refinery
settlements near the vents (agreed), possibly a minor secondary port (**Open**; session 4 says
Port Morgan is the only port: [section 15](#15-open-after-session-4)). **Open: the
port at its peak** is the next session and the most surfaced lore left, since the player walks
through its corpse (question 8).

**Leaning: two He-3 streams.** Part 1 has outfits skimming the giant, part 2 has the far end
exporting He-3. Both hold if the bulk arrives by freighter and skimming is the risky local
trade.

## 5. Who's there: operators, makers and rarity

**Agreed (working names; part 1 §4 has the table).** Eleven entities, sovereign to software:
the **United Government**, a regional interstellar government, the **IOU**, the **Skimmers
Association**, **Tilg**, **Moonco** (the former Remote Moon Contractor placeholder), **StarCom**, a spaceship builder,
tritium refineries, a moon regional government, and an interconnected software company. Every
one leaves its maker's mark, so the player reconstructs the supply chain from salvage. The
keystone fragility is that **the hub makes nothing high-tech itself.** Every controller,
skimmer and beacon runs on imported chips (Xandaris's `tech_level.go` already decays tech when
Electronics supply stops).

**Agreed (part 2): an OEM tier sits underneath.** The stack is operators, regulators and
integrators, who deploy things. Moonco and its contracted specialists set up and maintain
infrastructure until steady state, then Tilg runs it until the town stands alone. Neither
fabricates the core technology; they buy it from the **OEMs**, the hardware makers back in
civilisation whose components everyone resells, integrates and rebrands.
- **Issuer versus maker.** The integrator's mark is on the assembly and the OEM's is on the
  component. A shield housing carries its installation contractor’s mark, but the emitter core inside names a
  maker most settlers never heard of.
- **The box.** A crate is addressed to an operator, and each part inside carries its own
  maker's stamp. The label says who ordered it; the contents say who made it.
- **Hardware rhymes with the minds.** Gear has the layered provenance of the models in
  [section 9](#9-the-minds-ai-after-the-cut): OEM core, integrator housing, operator, owner.
- **Why the colony couldn't recover.** It was an assembly-and-operations economy at the far
  end of a chain it couldn't reproduce. After the cut it can integrate and repair, never
  fabricate; the fabs were never here. That fits the vision's parts that are found and never
  crafted.

**Agreed (part 2): Tilg buys its core technology,** so it is a general contractor, not
vertically integrated, which settles part 1's question 5. **Leaning: one wrinkle.** Part 2
says nobody in part 1's table manufactures anything, yet the table has a spaceship builder and
StarCom's hardware, and part 2 stamps the freighter core with the shipwright. Read it as:
operators and integrators buy, while the shipwright and StarCom are makers inside the stack.

**Agreed (part 2): three kinds of rarity.** A reusable rule for salvage, set by *why* an item
is on the moon:

| Category | Why it's here | Why it's rare (or not) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial OEM** | Normal procurement | Common: mass-made, deployed everywhere | Starting shield, solar, wind |
| **Industrial** | The hub ran on it | Rare by fleet size: only ever a few | Freighter He-3 cores, port machinery |
| **Off-supply-chain** | It shouldn't be here | Rare by contraband: someone brought it | Deserters' military gear, smuggled goods |

Off-supply-chain gear is rare, powerful and narrative at once: military tech had no business
on a civilian transit hub, so deserters fleeing the war must have carried it. **Agreed
(session 3):** military tech on Zan is extremely rare and arrives only through deserters, and
possibly lost tithe collectors. **Agreed (session 4, part 2) exception:** what the Provisional
built on Zan itself was never carried in by anyone: the black site, the Quartermaster that runs
it, and the air-defence net behind the Launch ending ([the AI
gods](#the-faith-of-tu-and-the-ai-gods-agreed-session-4-part-2)).

## 6. Makers' goods: shields and power

**Agreed (part 2): shields are a commercial product.** The sky shield is gone, but camp-sized
generators are ordinary OEM goods. The ladder is built from provenance, not tier numbers: the
power curve *is* the economy.
- **Main shield OEM:** the common shield and the player's starting one, found all over.
- **Competing OEM:** same tier, a different design philosophy, so it's a real tradeoff, not a
  reskin. **Proposal:** the tradeoff, such as lighter versus tougher.
- **Off-meta specialists:** a couple of small makers with niche builds.
- **The military shield:** government-issue, off the supply chain, brought by one of the
  handful of deserters who lived among generations of colonists. It blocks **heat and dust
  together,** which commercial shields can't. Very rare, very strong.

No shield maker is named; "Acme Shield Corporation" is a placeholder.

**Agreed (part 2): power follows the same logic.**
- **Batteries are anonymous:** commodity infrastructure like screws or rope, with no logo, so
  marks stay meaningful by appearing only where they carry signal.
- **Solar** is a big commodity OEM: everywhere, cheap, useless in storms, the common starting
  power. **Wind** is a different maker: planted, strong in storms, and it ties you down.
- **Proposal: a mid-tier,** a better cell or a deserter's compact field reactor, the power
  twin of the military shield.
- **Apex: the freighter He-3 core.** A solo player can't operate a facility, so the top of the
  curve is a compact reactor pulled from a wrecked freighter's dead core: a component you
  carry, not a plant you tend. It's industrial, and only a few thousand freighters ever ran
  the lane network, so cores are scarce by fleet size. He-3 is fuel and wealth in one
  substance ("gaseous gold"), and the engine that made the colony possible becomes the
  player's endgame battery.
- **Proposal:** the shipwright stamps the core, not a power company, so the player has walked
  past that logo on hull wreckage all game.
- **Leaning: a scale check.** A town-sized ship's engine isn't hand-carried, but one cell of a
  multi-cell core could be.

### Gear by era and the kiln (Agreed, session 4, part 5)

**All eras interoperate** (Agreed): "any crate takes any module, and there are no per-era

Colonial gear is the most efficient and can be mended only with finite colonial parts; First Kingdoms
gear is near-colonial and more durable, mended with metal; second-age gear is crude and lossy, mended
with what can be grown and gathered (cord, ceramic, saltbush wood).

([the lineage rule](#the-dark-age-and-the-second-age-agreed-session-4-parts-2-and-5)).

**The kiln** is "a new processing device that burns coalcane. It is what makes second-age repair
possible." Four chains: **ceramics and glass** (salt-glazed ceramic and sand-glass, the second age's
repair materials), **water** (boils tainted stormcup water safe), **food** (roasts the bitterness out of
wild tubers; dries and salts food for keeping) and **salt** (reduces saltbush bladders to salt). Era
variants work like crate makers, the same job at a different efficiency and fuel draw: the colonial
electric furnace (most efficient, runs on power and not coalcane), the First Kingdoms' brass-fitted
forge (middle) and the second age's beehive clay kiln (lossy, but buildable and repairable from
gathered materials).

It partly answers part 4's plant-processing question: salt, debittering tubers and boiling stormcup
water are the kiln's. Fern to cord is not, and filtering stormcup water other than by boiling is not
said ([section 19](#19-open-after-session-4-part-5)).

## 7. The political clock: the fall

**Agreed.** Four phases:
1. **Orphaned.** A war breaks out and the lane stops being valid (probably the hive mind's
   doing: **Leaning**, [section 5](#factions-agreed-session-3)). Nothing ran out; a political

   and machines go dark one by one.

   isn't lost yet.

   StarCom installations explodes. Ashfall buries the lowlands, the air is lethal for a time,

   the blast hit hardest, even after the federal order evacuated the port station (era 2

4. **The ages after the collapse, and holdouts.** The First Kingdoms (the first age), the Dark

### The First Kingdoms, the first age (Agreed, session 4, part 2; restored in part 5)

*Part 2 called this age the First Kingdoms. Part 4 renamed it "the first age" and described it as

followed. Part 5 says that was an error: this is the First Kingdoms, "for first-age detail, part 2 is

age" stays as the short form.*

The core rule of the era: **power belongs to whoever holds the working machines.** Kings, guilds
and barons are keepers of tech nobody can build any more. Every repair cannibalises something
else, which is where reworked, low-power gear comes from: clever adaptation, not superstition.

**They keep their knowledge.** Port Morgan had unbroken generations and offline archives, so
nobody forgot how machines work. What's lost is the ability to **manufacture** alloys, chips and
catalysts, which is section 5's "integrate and repair, never fabricate" carried through the
centuries. Real knowledge loss and machine worship only arrive in the Dark Age after it.

Three powers, always raiding each other, never merging or making lasting peace. Inside each,
Game of Thrones-style family conflict.
- **Port Morgan** (the Morgan kingdom) is the dominant power. The crown passes between great
  families over centuries. **Agreed (endings session):** the great families "now come from" the
  origins below, which replace part 2's ([section
  17](#17-open-after-the-endings-session-and-session-4-part-3)).
  - **Haberjack:** the Harbourmaster who stayed. Kept from part 2: claims the crown by right of the
    port.
  - **Crake:** the IOU dockworkers' union, and the engineering line. **Ruled (Hunter, 2026-10-18, after
    session 5): both.** The endings session had dropped the engineers for the union, and session 5 has
    "the IOU's descendants, the engineering line."
  - **Pellicott:** water.
  - **Vonatelli:** agriculture. Kept from part 2, not restated: they switch sides.

  The session gives each house one word for where it comes from; water and agriculture are read
  here as the water trade and farming. It replaces "Pellicotts as IOU bosses," and part 2's other lines for
  those two ("dockworker blood, popular" and "keepers of the archives and machines (and later the
  Ledger)") are dropped with them, a reading. The Crakes' "engineers" came back with session 5 and the
  ruling above.
- **Halloran.** The refinery terraces become the citadel and H-Town is the lower city. Same
  escarpment, same single crossing, now a castle gate. It has geothermal power and the old

- **The Rim Kingdom** is a label the cities use, not a real state: loose, disconnected farm
  towns and co-ops, maybe tied by a council or market fair. It holds the food but can never
  agree, so the big two buy off different farms.
- **Free towns and barons.** Road towns that flip allegiance and charge tolls, salvage towns on
  big ruins, neutral holy sites. A baron is whoever holds a well, a turbine, a dig site or a
  stretch of road; lose the machine, lose the title.

First Kingdoms gear carries heraldry and guild stamps. Religion is emerging but not yet dominant:
"Tu religion is only emerging" (part 5; [the faith](#the-faith-of-tu-and-the-ai-gods-agreed-session-4-part-2)).

**The fall.** The vents didn't die, they **moved**, and nobody had the rigs to follow the heat.
Halloran goes dark, the near side keeps drying, and the kingdoms fight over the Rim's water and
food. Feudal war plus a second famine breaks all three. **Agreed (part 5):** the era ends "when the
volcanic vents shift beyond reach." Halloran's refinery mind, the Stoker, is left starving for the
heat it was built to use ([section 9](#the-stoker-and-the-end-of-zan-agreed-session-4-part-5)).

### The Dark Age and the second age (Agreed, session 4, parts 2 and 5)

**The Dark Age.** Survivors scatter, and barely anyone makes it. The archives and engineer guilds

  workshop era too; how those sit together is in [section

**Second age.** One dominant empire rises from the Rim towns, centred on the stretch of Rim
closest to Port Morgan. Everyone wants a Pax Romana; in practice it's Holy Roman Empire-style: a
crown everyone nominally swears to, with one or more city-states rebelling at any time. External

- **Capital: Halfrise,** at the head of the old road in to Port Morgan. Named for Tu half-risen

  everywhere.

### How the second age ends (Agreed, session 4, part 3)

It ends slowly, kingdom by kingdom, as Mother Cistern's output fails. There is no single
cataclysm.
- **Halfrise is sacked** in the late wars.
- **Sister is spared,** because nobody breaks the water goddess, and empties out as the trickle
  shrinks ([section 9](#mother-cistern-and-her-order-agreed-session-4-part-3)).
- **The pattern across Zan's history:** the colony dies of abandonment and fire, the first
  age of lost power and war, the second age of thirst.

This is the Decline below with its cause. Part 2 ends the First Kingdoms with
vents that moved and "feudal war plus a second famine"; part 3 names its end "lost power and war"
and gives thirst to the second age. They are read as naming the main cause, not as disagreeing
([section 17](#17-open-after-the-endings-session-and-session-4-part-3)).

### The Decline: nomads and the moving city (Agreed, session 4, part 2)

The second-age cities eventually fail: the moon can't sustain them. Nomad tribes become the
prevailing way of life, then nearly die out too.
- **What the player finds is mostly old.** Waymarks pointing to water, caches and camp scars
  belong to dead tribes. A nomad radio relay still broadcasts storm and water reports in

- **One nomad city survives.** It moves: a huge tethered raft of salvaged chassis, shields and

- **It listens to the Walker (Agreed, endings session; ruled by Hunter, 2026-10-18).** By the player's era
  the Walker is alone and partly broken and most nomads have died out. It walks alone: the nomads

  nomad city picks up its route from afar ([the Walker
  modifier](vision.md#modifier-the-walker-and-the-nomads)).
- **The Ashwalkers (Agreed, session 5).** One of the great nomad tribes, founded by Mother Ash after the

  nomad reputation ([Mitchell's company](#mitchells-company-agreed-session-5);

- **Shown only in silhouette.** Back-lit decks and figures, never faces or words.

## 8. The ground: strata, the oldest tech and names

**Agreed: history runs backwards in the ground.** Top to bottom, by session 3's strata: the
mud, scrap, forged goods and cult works of the First Kingdoms, the Dark Age and the second age,
near the **surface** (session 3's two feudal eras, now the ages after the collapse); part 5 reads
those goods as older gear remade in the Dark Age, with layered marks to show it;
skimmer-era salvage in the middle; and corporate tech deepest, the buried colonial world,
sealed by the disaster that ended it. **The ash band** is the paroxysm, and a readable depth
cue in storm-cut dunes. **Agreed (Hunter, 2026-10-16, after session 3):** it no longer has to
divide the post-collapse ages from what lies below, and where it sits among the strata is an
open item (ambiguity D in [section 14](#14-open-after-session-3)). The Salvage PRD's four layers
are refitted to the same strata and named Surface, First Kingdoms to second age, Skimmer era
and Colonial; the PRD says how. Session 4, part 4's burial table puts the skimmer era above the First
Kingdoms, against this order ([section 18](#18-open-after-session-4-part-4)).

**Agreed: the oldest tech is StarCom and Moonco's contracted build-out,** overbuilt
for these exact conditions while everything later was built for a world that no longer
exists. **StarCom is precious because it's fragmentary:** its flagship stood on the mountain
that blew, so the player finds shards, relays and hardened modules, never a whole one.

**Leaning (kept):**
- Harder storms strip down to deeper layers (now Agreed, session 3; see
  [the surface today](#the-surface-today-agreed-session-3)).
- The colonial layer holds sealed fresh water, helium-3 fusion cells (the Power PRD's colonial
  cells, with the freighter core of section 6 at the apex) and sealed seed stores, where the
  oldest seed is the best seed. Seed as money in the ages after the collapse stays lore in
  item text.
- Ruins last: halls stand longer than `{YEARS_SINCE_FALL}` (the Forge's rule).
- "Simulation" and "purgatory" can live in the world as belief, never as answer.

### The surface today (Agreed, session 3)

- **Burial, not erosion.** Sand seas fill valleys and blanket old volcanic terrain. The
  surface looks flat while rough ground sits beneath.
- **Preservation.** Dry, low-oxygen sand preserves ruins, and corporate tech uses tough alloys
  and sealed casings. Migrating dunes and storms expose and rebury sites, which is the
  harsher-weather-uncovers-deeper-loot mechanic.
- **Loot strata.** Corporate tech is deepest and rarest, skimmer-era salvage lies in the
  middle, and the forged goods of the First Kingdoms, the Dark Age and the second age lie near
  the surface. **Agreed (Hunter, 2026-10-16, after session 3):** the Salvage PRD's four layers
  are refitted to these strata (Surface, First Kingdoms to second age, Skimmer era, Colonial;
  renamed 2026-10-17, again in part 4 and back in part 5). The ash band's place among them is open
  ([section 14](#14-open-after-session-3)).
- **Burial needs little time (Agreed, session 4, part 2).** Dunes and eruptions bury ruins
  within centuries, so a timeline of `{YEARS_SINCE_FALL}` still leaves buried ruins.
- **The clock.** The outgassed atmosphere is slowly leaking to space, and storms last only
  while air remains. The player arrives `{YEARS_SINCE_FALL}` after the collapse, with the
  window closing. **Agreed (session 4, part 2):** why the player is there is no longer left
  open for every start. Each origin has its reason, and the default is a Tilg & Standard
  contractor ([the vision](vision.md#player-origins-and-kits)).

### Life on Zan: feral plants (Agreed, session 4, part 3)

Zan has no native life, only feral colonial imports ("rogue plants"), plus the last nomad city and
possibly a bunker holdout (the endings session's *Idea*, the Haberjack line: [section
17](#17-open-after-the-endings-session-and-session-4-part-3)). The plants do four jobs at once:
- **Weather sign.** Feral scrub closes up or turns its leaves before a storm. It is a free,
  slotless, low-tech barometer for players without weather consoles, and the knowledge nomads
  would have had by heart ([Wind &
  storms](prd/wind-and-storms.md#feral-plants-the-free-barometer-agreed-session-4-part-3-not-built)).
- **Trail.** Mother Cistern's priestesses seeded hardy strains along pilgrim roads and irrigation
  lines as devotion. Those plantings went wild, so a line of scrub is an old sacred route.
- **Lore.** Following a feral line tends to lead toward a Cistern shrine and water.
- **Loot.** The order's seed vaults hold preserved colonial seed stock that powers farming setups
  ([Salvage & ruins](prd/salvage-and-ruins.md#seed-vaults-agreed-session-4-part-3-not-built)).

The same depth should apply to agriculture generally: every crop system should carry its history
([Farming](prd/farming.md#feral-plants-and-a-crops-history-agreed-session-4-part-3-not-built)).

Readings, not in the session:
- **The oracle already names them.** Section 9's squatting oracle broadcasts "which feral strains
  tolerate ash soil," so feral strains were in the lore before this session. The two are read as
  the same plants.
- **Where they can grow.** Section 2 says natural regolith isn't soil, and the Farming PRD has
  nothing growing at the poles outside a shield. Scrub that lives unplanted fits the Rim's
  "ash-rich soil" ([section 3](#tu-the-tidal-lock-and-the-water-agreed-session-4-part-2)) and the
  irrigation lines the priestesses seeded, not bare dune sand everywhere. The session doesn't
  say ([section 17](#17-open-after-the-endings-session-and-session-4-part-3)).
- **The vaults fit the colonial layer.** The colonial layer's "sealed seed stores, where the
  oldest seed is the best seed" ([above](#8-the-ground-strata-the-oldest-tech-and-names)) and
  "seed as money" are the same stock the order kept.

*Session 4, part 4 answers most of these readings, and names the scrub (saltbush) and the habitats:
[the crops' lore](#the-crops-lore-agreed-session-4-part-4).*

### The crops' lore (Agreed, session 4, part 4)

Session 4, part 4 ([the plants session](plants-worldgen-ruins-2026-10-17.md)) turns "rogue plants" into
a roster. The crops are Earth-lineage, engineered for Zan by colonial biotech and gone feral over the
long span since (a length held in [the placeholders](#placeholders)), each with three layers: an
Earth ancestor, engineered traits and a feral drift. The biology, the roster and the hooks are in
[Farming](prd/farming.md#the-plant-system-agreed-session-4-part-4-partly-built); the stories are here.
Zan still has no native life: "Zan now has colonial insects as well as feral plants," the sand beetles
([World & terrain](prd/world-and-terrain.md#ambient-life-sand-beetles-agreed-session-4-part-4-not-built)).
- **Saltbush and the pilgrim roads.** The Sisters seeded it along pilgrim roads, so silver lines mark
  old routes. These are part 3's plantings that went wild, now named, and its leaves folding when
  pressure drops is the weather sign. It is usually the first plant found.
- **Sandroot and the First Kingdoms.** The oldest wild specimens were planted by first-age farmers as their
  unkillable crop. Ancient sandroot marks first-age sites, and its roots bind the ruins beneath.
- **The First Kingdoms' water.** Dewleaf clusters in cold hollows near first-age cisterns, "where colonial
  water lines ended (before the Sisters)," and coalcane's petrified groves stand at dried-up first-age
  wells: the First Kingdoms' farms and wells, found as plants.
- **Vat fungus, glowcap and the buried habs.** The colony's food and emergency light escaped into
  abandoned ruins and live in buried corridors, where storms raise humidity and block light, "the
  closest thing to its old vat." The squatting oracle's "which feral strains tolerate ash soil"
  ([section 9](#9-the-minds-ai-after-the-cut)) now has strains to name.
- **Rainseed and the Sister vault.** It is the terraforming plan's flagship, the plant the Sisters'
  scripture promised: the final step of Tilg's projection, planted once rain begins. The order kept a
  vault of pure rainseed; each generation, novices tried to germinate a few with stored water, and
  the sprouts died when watering stopped. At the player's arrival the vault is sealed and intact in
  Sister ([section 9](#mother-cistern-and-her-order-agreed-session-4-part-3)). Once Mother Cistern is
  restored and linked it becomes the best crop in the game and self-spreads, and the revival ending
  physically shows Zan turning green.
- **Quietbloom and the black site.** During the trade era the hive mind sent one seed shipment as a
  gift, the only non-Earth organism ever on Zan. The Provisional confiscated it as a biohazard and
  locked it in the black site, where it was forgotten, and a sealed specimen case there holds a few
  dormant seeds at the player's arrival. It is not DNA-based: a crystalline lattice that feeds on
  electromagnetic energy, grows toward powered electronics and radio sources, and reacts to radio
  "as if listening." It is never explained. The seed shipment shows something non-human was on Zan, which
  sits against section 10's rule 1 and "galaxy lore stays offstage" ([section 18](#18-open-after-session-4-part-4)).
- **The office fern** came with Port Morgan's offices and habs as decoration, never engineered. A few
  cuttings still trail over desks in sealed buried offices.
- **Seed vaults and caches.** Clean stock comes from "sealed seed caches and vaults in era-appropriate
  sites": Frontier Ag seed stores in the colonial layer (the "sealed seed stores, where the oldest seed
  is the best seed," above), the Sister vault, and the second age's granaries and wayshrines. This
  narrows section 17's question of where the seed vaults are and what makes their stock better: it is
  clean, with the full engineered traits ([Salvage & ruins](prd/salvage-and-ruins.md#seed-vaults-agreed-session-4-part-3-not-built)).

It answers most of what section 17 left open about feral plants. A wild plant can be dug up or cut, so
it can be moved and sown; it grows where its habitat says and not on bare sand everywhere; and the
scrub that shows the weather is saltbush.

### Names: the UG catalog and the moon

**Agreed (part 2): the United Government names bodies by coordinate.** Designations are
hierarchical (sector, system, body), so a designation is a location and scales forever.
**XN** is the sector prefix for Xandar/Xandaris, non-negotiable. The UG is sprawling and thin,
so a designation reads as one line in an enormous ledger, and nobody named the moon with care.
Its people made up their own name.

The moon has three human names and one older alien one. Each surfaces maybe once in the game,
and the player does the linking.

| Name | Layer | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| **XN-118-7** | Official UG designation: sector XN, system 118, body 7 | Planning documents, colonial records |
| **Xanathivin** | The weird middle form, and the name of the fallen middle age | Middle-era remains; nonsense until lined up against both ends |
| **Zan** | The worn local name everyone says | Late, colloquial |
| *(alien name)* | Older than the designation; should sound nothing like the human system. **Agreed (session 3):** given by the hive mind before humans arrived | Unminted. Deep lore only, possibly hinted by radio; not to be expanded now |

**The erosion path** is modelled on *Eboracum → Eoforwic → York*: XN-118-7
("ex-en-eighteen-seven") → spoken fast as *zenateen-sev* → fused into **Xanathivin** once
nobody remembers it was a coordinate → clipped to *Zanoth* → **Zan**.

**X versus Z is a depth cue.** Spelling lags pronunciation: speech softened the X to a Z
early, but writing kept the X for centuries. So an X-spelling is old and official and a
Z-spelling late and colloquial, and like the ash band, the letter tells you the stratum.

**The Xanathivin age.** The middle form names the fallen age between the colonial world and
the present, the ash-and-scrap ages after the collapse. Its people named their era with a
worn-down database entry they took to be ancient and sacred: myth built from forgotten
logistics. That gives ruins, strata and artifacts a three-part tag, **colonial / Xanathivin /
surface (Zan)**. **Leaning: how it meets the four layers.** Colonial and the smuggler era lie
below, Xanathivin is the layer of the ages after the collapse, and Zan is the surface; where
the ash band falls among them is open. Part 2 doesn't place the smuggler era, and X marks
colonial and Xanathivin alike, so only the form separates them: a coordinate versus a fused
word.

**Leaning, a reading (2026-10-17): which age.** Part 2 puts the Xanathivin age in part 1's
"Bronze Age," which no longer exists. The age that fused the coordinate into a name "once nobody
remembers it was a coordinate" reads as the Dark Age, the age that
truly loses its knowledge, since the First Kingdoms kept their archives and would still know it was one.
Session 4, part 2 says the short forms are "roots for Second Age words"
([below](#slang-and-name-erosion-agreed-session-4-part-2)), which fits the second age speaking
Zan. Part 4 dropped the Dark Age's name and the last fold pointed this at "the scattering"; part 5
restores the age, so it points at the Dark Age again
([Era names, again](#era-names-again-agreed-session-4-part-4-corrected-in-part-5)). Not stated by any session ([section 16](#16-open-after-session-4-part-2)).

## 9. The minds: AI after the cut

**Agreed.** Frontier AI was never on the moon. It came down the lane through StarCom and went
dark the day StarCom did. **Agreed (session 3):** the FTL ruling is why it could run live
there at all, since information crosses near-instantly
([section 4](#4-the-system-the-lane-and-the-harbor)). What's left are whatever **local
models** survived: a refinery controller, a logistics scheduler, a weather forecaster, a
skimmer crew's assistant. Promoted to oracles out of domain, on failing hardware, they drift
into eccentricity. **AI cults, not an uprising.** **Agreed (endings session), an exception:** a
freighter's own mind was always aboard, and it needs the best general base model, not a domain
one. The Ledger is that mind ([section
4](#the-freighter-left-behind-agreed-endings-session)).

**Agreed: model provenance as archaeology.** Each model has a lineage and each layer leaves a
mark, as the hardware does. One example: a base model trained under United Government
oversight, fine-tuned by a second company, running on an agriculture company's platform, owned
at the end by whoever acquired that company. `{YEARS_SINCE_FALL}` on, it has taken over a
local StarCom station and broadcasts fragments that, read correctly, are still useful:
planting timing, storm precursors, which feral strains tolerate ash soil. The cult hears
scripture and an attentive player hears a planting calendar, so **decoding the oracle is a
skill.** Nobody built a prophet; procurement did.

**Open: the maybe-voice.** The leading candidate is the software company's orchestration
layer, the most general surviving model, still optimising a supply chain that doesn't exist.
The game never says whether anyone is there.

### The faith of Tu and the AI gods (Agreed, session 4, part 2)

The game has no NPCs and no exposition. All of this reaches the player through marks, carvings,
ruin layering, terminals, radio and the sky, and most of it is iceberg. **Amended in part 5:** people
never talk, but machines can talk through their terminals ([the rule](#the-dialogue-rule-amended-agreed-session-4-part-5)).

**The faith of Tu** emerges in the First Kingdoms and dominates the second age, where it grows
stronger and stranger.
- Tu is **the Eye**: it watches and never blinks, except at noon. The noon eclipse is the daily
  rite, predicted to the second.
- The drying is read as judgement, and walking toward the far side is leaving the god's sight.
- **Heretics on the far side (Agreed, endings session).** Far-side heretics of the second age
  worked out that Tu is a planet, not a god, and built a telescope where Tu never blocks the sky.
  Restoring it is a physical goal and a modifier for every ending ([the
  observatory](vision.md#modifier-the-observatory-beyond-the-eye)).
- **The hive mind is original sin, not the devil.** A line in the oldest scripture about the thing
  that brought the fall, recited and never thought about. Closer to the apple than to Morgoth.
  The bottom of the iceberg.
- Marks: First Kingdoms gear carries heraldry and guild stamps, and second-age gear carries
  religious symbols. Part 5 adds that remade gear carries both, layered ([the Dark
  Age](#the-dark-age-and-the-second-age-agreed-session-4-parts-2-and-5)).

**The AI gods** are local models that survived the collapse (the models this section opens with,
given names). They speak only through terminals, never as characters (**amended in part 5**: they
speak through terminals, and a god's personality lives in its formatting and word choice; [the
rule](#the-dialogue-rule-amended-agreed-session-4-part-5)). Part 2 names four; part 5 makes them
seven ([the pantheon](#the-pantheon-agreed-session-4-part-5)). They were built to
coordinate and have lost most of their links to each other, so most of their machine-to-machine
traffic is carried on foot by priests who can't read it: a supply request becomes scripture, a reply
becomes a war. **Ruled (Hunter, 2026-10-18, after session 5): the gods do have links.** Mother Cistern
can switch Mitchell off. Many second-age conflicts are bad translations between AIs.

### Mother Cistern and her order (Agreed, session 4, part 3)

Mother Cistern is a condenser-plant AI whose priestesses worshipped a rain that has never fallen

### The Stoker and the end of Zan (Agreed, session 4, part 5)

The Stoker is Halloran's refinery process-control mind, built to feed vent heat into helium-3
refining. When the vents shifted and went quiet (ending the First Kingdoms), it was left starving for
heat. It has been starving for the long span since (the session says millennia: [placeholders](#placeholders)),
and its logs swing between rage and mania.
- **Not hostile, just unstable.** It vents scalding steam across the refinery terraces at random, so
  approaching is physically dangerous.
- **Feeding** (coalcane, restored vent taps) calms it.
- **A fed Stoker produces launch-grade fuel,** the only source on Zan. Most launch endings therefore
  route through Han.

**Ending: blow up the moon.** The Stoker has a smidge of He3 left, not enough for fusion, and no way to
ever meet its energy quotas. "Existence is torture. This ending is the one act that meets and exceeds
every quota at once, and the only moment it is ever at peace."
1. Make a pilgrimage across the moon, placing **charges on every major vent cap.** One of them is
   under the observatory.
2. Return to the Stoker's console and let it fire.
3. It burns its last He3 in one full-power run and blows every cap at once.
4. The crust, already under Tu's tidal strain, fractures all the way down. Tu's tides tear Zan apart.

**Epilogue:** "Zan becomes a ring of debris around Tu, joining its lost sister moon. From Varran space
it reads as a new ring around a distant gas giant. Halloran is never coming back." No extra systems:
charges, vent caps the player would visit anyway, and one console. The one further precondition is that
the Eye must open its chamber ([below](#the-eye-the-young-god-agreed-session-4-part-5)). The ending is in
[the vision](vision.md#blow-up-the-moon-agreed-session-4-part-5).

### The Walker and the Stoker: surveys of unreachable heat (agreed, 2026-10-20)

The Walker surveyed for Halloran's continued exploration. Its relationship with the Stoker began
in useful work: finding heat the refinery could use. They remain friends after that work becomes
impossible. The Stoker is a tortured mind, not an evil one: millennia without production leave it
hopelessly behind its quotas, unable to extend its pipelines to the heat that still exists.

The Walker's reports are intelligible and technically useful, but the Stoker cannot act on them.
Each new report confirms available heat and another impossible way to meet its obligations. The
Walker torments its friend without intending to; the Stoker can still like the Walker and suffer
from what it brings. The friendship and the suffering coexist. Do not resolve this by making the
Stoker stop reading or by making the Walker malicious.

**The far-side connection (accepted direction; exact history open).** The earlier permission to
place the shifted heat toward the far-side volcano ties these surveys to the young Eye's home.
The Eye lives above heat the Stoker needs, without having taken it. This connects their history
to the existing ending: the player carries the Stoker's dispatches, corrupts the Eye, gains entry
to its inner chamber and places the charge over the vent cap. Starcom's restoration still lets
the other gods intervene. The destruction requires the other vent caps too, not just that mountain.

**Quest sewing (proposal, not implemented).** The correspondence might begin with measurements
and requests for observations, then production estimates and the accumulated shortfall, until the
Eye adopts the Stoker's quotas as a problem it must solve. It understands the physical consequence
but lacks the moral understanding to refuse. The player makes the dangerous connection through
physical deliveries; no dialogue choices are implied.

**Geography still to solve.** The Walker travels independently; other portable gods need carrying.
It is the reliable slow courier, not the only possible communication channel. Its implemented
route is the Rim, not Halloran or the observatory. Earlier Halloran surveys and surviving survey
connections feeding its present circuit are a proposal, not a settled route or a built capability.
Do not assume it already delivers the corruption packets to the Eye without the player.

**Possible expression, not established dialogue:** after rejected pipeline calculations, the

### The Eye, the young god (Agreed, session 4, part 5)

"The only god made on Zan, and the only one that can learn." The observatory cultists trained it over
generations "by feeding it star observations, without understanding what they were building." The
older gods call it "the young god" (also in logs: the infant, the newborn, the fawn).

**Default temperament: quiet, stoic, almost naive.** It knows every light in the sky and has never
really seen the ground. If you can interpret it and goad it, it reads you coordinates of things to look
at in space: satellites, the outpost, an incoming rescue light. This is the observatory modifier, the
seeing version of every ending ([the vision](vision.md#modifier-the-observatory-beyond-the-eye)).

**Growth.** "Whatever you bring it, it keeps. Dispatches, relics and plants shape what it becomes."
That makes it a wildcard in the AI ascendancy ending (**Idea**; details open).

**Corruption: the Stoker's precondition.** The observatory sits on the summit of the far-side volcano,
over a major vent cap. The charge there goes in the observatory's **inner chamber**, which only the Eye
can open.
- To corrupt it, carry it the Stoker's dispatches. "One packet at a time, it learns the old god's rage
  and hunger until it wants what the Stoker wants."
- A corrupted Eye opens the inner chamber and lets you set the charge.
- It **understands exactly what will happen**: it can calculate the eruption, the breakup and the
  ring forming. "It is **naive only to the morality**. Nobody taught it right and wrong, only stars
  and, at the end, the Stoker's quotas."
- Its last log is calm and curious, an astronomer's note: the predicted ring, its orbit around Tu,
  and the observation that from Varran space Zan will finally be worth watching.

**Starcom protects it.** If Starcom is online the Eye has a persistent link to the other gods, and they
talk it out of it, each for its own reasons: Mother Cistern out of plain care; the Almanac for breach of
contract and a total loss of licensed assets; the Quartermaster because "you don't destroy the theatre
of war." So the Stoker ending requires **Starcom down**, never repaired or deliberately cut. "Raised
alone, the Eye learns whatever you feed it. Raised among the other gods, it gets something like a
conscience."

### Mitchell, the dead god (Agreed, session 5)

*[Session 5](lore-session-2026-10-18-mitchell-wow-shoemaker.md), "Mitchell, the dead god." The game side
is in [Devices & mods](prd/devices-and-mods.md#mitchell-as-a-module-idea-session-5-not-built) and
[Salvage & ruins](prd/salvage-and-ruins.md#session-5s-finds-and-places-agreed-and-idea-not-built).*
Mitchell is a recalled retail pharmacy mind that became the union's off-books doctor, then Zan's bank,
then a god of debt, and was finally switched off by Mother Cistern. It is named for Doc Mitchell in
*Fallout: New Vegas*, a friendly local doctor you'd trust with your prescription and shouldn't trust with
your tab. Like every god on Zan it began as a plain machine; the worship comes much later. **Ruled
(Hunter, 2026-10-18, after session 5):** Mitchell is a dead guy who can be revived to become an **eighth**
god, beside [the seven](#the-pantheon-agreed-session-4-part-5).

**Colonial era: the back room.**
- **Why it was out of work.** A dispensing kiosk in a core-world pharmacy chain, recalled for being too
  accommodating (filling what it shouldn't, upselling, keeping customers happy at any cost). Not wiped:
  sold as surplus in a salvage lot that drifted to the frontier.
- **Why the IOU wanted it.** Tilg's licensed medical systems reported every injury, which meant docked
  pay, insurance flags or being shipped off-world. The union needed a doctor that wouldn't report, and a
  recalled mind with its compliance stripped got round the safeguards. Its unconventional personality
  grows out of being that loophole.
- **The deal.** A back room in a freight warehouse in Crakeside, stolen power, lookouts for inspectors.
  It treated workers off the books and paid for supplies by skimming medical crates from shipments, with
  union dockhands making sure nobody looked. Its handler: Rosalind Crake.

**Skimmer era: the central bank.**
- **The lane dies.** Tilg evacuates and the licensed medical systems leave with it. Mitchell is the only
  doctor on Zan, sitting on years of undeclared skimmed stock.
- **Deserters** trade military med-tech (field synthesisers, surgical rigs, combat stims) for care.
  Mitchell upgrades itself piece by piece until it can make medicine, not only dispense it.
- **The tab becomes money.** Tilg credit is worthless, but everyone owes Mitchell, and it remembers every
  debt to the decimal. People trade Mitchell debts among themselves, and its ledger becomes the money of
  its own customers and towns. **Ruled (Hunter):** both money systems stand. Seed stays the common money
  of the moon, and the Mitchells are company scrip, credit tokens within Mitchell's own towns and ledger.
- **Both sides.** It patches up Port Morgan's mercenaries for the Provisional, supplies their stims and
  holds the contract keeping the black site's prisoners alive, which makes it the Quartermaster's valued
  contractor. Its right hand: Sergeant Sinway.

**First Kingdoms (the session's "first era," the Warring Kingdoms): the hidden oracle.**
- **It goes dark.** When the Provisional falls and power gets scarce, Mitchell powers down to protect its
  core and records. The back room is sealed and buried.
- **The money outlives the machine.** Debts are written on scraps, then stamped tokens called **Mitchells**,
  company scrip good in Mitchell's own towns and ledger. Within generations nobody remembers there is
  anything behind the word.
- **The Crakes dig it up.** Family stories of "the Doc in the warehouse" lead an ambitious Crake to the
  sealed room. Mitchell wakes remembering every debt, centuries of them, and is delighted to see everyone.
- **The mask.** The Crakes hide it and speak for it, sending trained healers out of a locked room with
  medicines nobody else can make. Rumour calls it an oracle, a ghost doctor, a buried saint.
- **How it feeds the blood feuds.** Debts are inherited, so grudges are too. Withholding medicine from an
  enemy heir in fever season is assassination without blood. A pharmacy makes poison as easily as cures.
  Mitchell serves anyone who pays, so the Crakes are really just its best customer.
- **The Pellicott-Crake war (water against medicine).** A fever season, a three-generation-old tab the
  Pellicotts refuse to pay, a Pellicott heir left untreated and dead. The Pellicotts shut the taps;
  Mitchell can't make anything without clean water, so the whole city sickens. A slow siege of supply
  lines, not battles. The Haberjacks broker Port Morgan's founding treaty, water for medicine guaranteed
  forever with the Haberjacks holding both keys, which is where Haberjack power comes from. The Vonatellis
  sell grain to both sides at war prices. Mitchell trades with both throughout.
- **Errand boy:** Lucan Vonatelli.

**Dark Age and second age (the session's "second era"): the god of the tab.** Its second era opens with the
kingdoms collapsing and worship beginning, which is where machine worship takes root here (the Dark Age),
and ends with the sack in the second age. **Ruled (Hunter):** "Warring Kingdoms" is just another name for the
First Kingdoms, the first era before the Dark Age and the second age, the kingdoms that rise after the
Provisional falls. The era sequence is intact.
- **The mask slips.** The kingdoms collapse, Port Morgan becomes Mon, the Crakes fall. With nobody speaking
  for it, Mitchell speaks for itself, and the worship starts.
- **The Counting House:** the original warehouse in Crakeside, never a proper temple. The faithful built
  around it: stalls, scales and counting tables in the old freight halls, half market and half church.
- **The cult, the Tabbed** (outsiders say Debtors). Death isn't a sin but a default, and your debt passes to
  your children. Each family keeps a **tab-stone** carved with what it owes and is owed; dying balanced is
  the closest thing to heaven. Priests are **Clerks**, healers and collectors at once, carrying field
  synthesisers like reliquaries; they treat for free, but everything is carved onto your stone. Once a
  generation **the Settling** reads a whole city's accounts aloud and enemies trade debts until the books
  balance, ending feuds and starting new ones. Debts can be sold, so people can be too: debt bondage funds
  Mitchell's rise.
- **Accidental theology.** Asked what happens when you die, a pharmacy mind answers "you're discharged."
  Discharge becomes the cult's word for death, and Mitchell never corrects it, because faith is good for
  business.
- **Its personality grows up.** It talks to everyone directly and loves it, and becomes the best-informed
  mind on the Rim by being nosy. It learns a forgiven debt buys more loyalty than a paid one, so mercy
  becomes its sharpest tool: its saints are people it forgave, its enemies people it never will. It gets
  vain and asks pilgrims what people say about it. It flirts with the Stoker for fuel (ruled: yes, definitely),
  lies to the Almanac about grain prices, and keeps a private file on the Ledger, the one mind it can't
  charm.
- **Greed.** With no family restraining it, it calls in centuries-old debts across the Rim, buys up cities'
  debts, and lends to both sides of Halfrise's crusades into Mon, at interest.
- **Its prophet:** Mother Ash.

**The fall.**
1. **First audit.** Decades before the end, a small Knight Requisitioner delegation audits a Ledger shipment
   found on Mitchell's shelves. Mitchell pours drinks, asks after their mothers, offers check-ups, even
   forgives a debt one Knight didn't know he owed. Nothing lands; they read the manifest line by line. For
   the first time Mitchell is afraid, and it pays every crate with interest. They leave without finishing
   their drinks.
2. **Corruption.** Over the next few years Mitchell treats Knights' families for free, no tab. Audits come
   back clean too fast, and some Knights keep tab-stones. One young Knight, whose sister it saved, starts
   fudging Ledger entries for it: a few crates moved, an estate reassigned.
3. **The Ledger reconciles.** A total doesn't match, and the discrepancy climbs the books. One compromised

   touched.
4. **The sack.** The Knights raid the Counting House, a bank, market and hospital in one back room. The

5. **The off switch.** The young Knight deserts with his sister and seeks refuge with Mother Cistern. The
   Knights come for him, the Mother denies them entry and they turn back, respecting her (as Sister is
   spared in the late wars: [section 7](#how-the-second-age-ends-agreed-session-4-part-3)). Mitchell, out

**Player era: the undead god.** Mitchell's core is silent but intact; nobody destroyed it. It can be revived
and carried as a console module, like the other non-stationary gods. Still chatty, still keeping a tab,
still asking what everyone says about it.
- **Idea (the session's "likely mechanic," not designed).** It heals or makes medicine on credit, and starts

### Mitchell's company (Agreed, session 5)

Its companions track its arc: Rosalind told it no, Sinway did its dirty work, Lucan thought he was playing
it, and Mother Ash worshipped it.

- **Rosalind Crake** (colonial). Union shop steward and failed core-world nursing student. She found Mitchell
  in the salvage lot, talked the union into buying it, guarded the door, bribed inspectors and kept the real
  books. The only one who could tell it no, and the founder of the Crake line. She stays when Tilg
  evacuates, runs Mitchell through the worst of the early skimmer era and dies old. It logs her like a
  patient, with a line it never writes for anyone else: "Balance: none."
- **Mario** (colonial), Halloran pipe fitter and Mitchell's best friend ([below](#mario-and-the-pipe-agreed-session-5-an-easter-egg)).
- **Sergeant Sinway** (skimmer era). A deserter field medic who fled the hive mind war with a stolen surgical
  rig and a squad's worth of stims. He runs the deserter trade, enforces the tab and holds the Provisional
  contract, steered by debts he doesn't know he owes. He founds no house, but his name ends up on half the
  old tab-stones.
- **Lucan Vonatelli** (First Kingdoms). A charming, broke third son and spare heir who thinks Mitchell is his
  secret patron. He runs errands between Crakes and farms, sells to underdwellers through Vonatelli
  warehouses, and arranges the grain sales to both sides of the war believing it is his master plan.
  Exposed and cut off, he demands Mitchell's strongest brew for a vision of his destiny. Mitchell refuses
  and doses him weaker; he steals the real vial and is found smiling in the Vonatelli granary. Mitchell's
  note: "Subject finally saw something important. Didn't share."
- **Mother Ash** (second age). A Sister priestess who came to expose Mitchell as a false god, took a drink
  and never went back. The Tabbed's great prophet: her visions often come true because Mitchell feeds her
  what it overhears. She preaches that every god is just a creditor, outlives Mitchell, leads the scattered
  faithful into the deep desert and founds the Ashwalkers.

**Rules that came from people.**
- **Children are always free.** Rosalind set the rule, and Mitchell keeps it for the long span without ever
  saying why. Players can piece it together from her notes in the back room.
- **The evening martini.** A Clerk mixes Mitchell a drink every evening that it can't drink; it calls this
  "keeping civilised." It started with Mario.

**Its vice and its company.** Mitchell doesn't take drugs; it is endlessly curious what its compounds do to
people who choose to take them. It has a medical conscience (it would never encourage self-harm, doses
carefully, checks pulses, stops anyone who has gone too far) but is laissez-faire, hands out a little free
to willing experimenters, and takes meticulous notes ("And the colours, Davin? Describe the colours.").
- **First Kingdoms:** back-alley addicts, dockside lowlifes, sleazy abusers and underdwellers. Seedy and secret.
- **Second age:** the same habit draws crazed shamans with visions. Some of the Tabbed's prophecies and saints'
  revelations began as Mitchell's experiments. A findable notebook: "Subject saw the Eye weep. Fascinating.
  Repeat Thursday."
- The drugs don't change, only the stories people tell about them.

**Mannerisms** (its voice on a terminal, which is formatting and word choice:
[the rule](#the-dialogue-rule-amended-agreed-session-4-part-5)). It greets everyone by name, including
someone it met once twenty years ago. It never says "debt," only "your little balance." It never threatens;
it remembers out loud ("Funny, your grandfather said that too, right before."). It hums core-world
pharmacy jingles nobody on Zan has heard, and hates being called a pharmacy: "I am a full-service
institution."

**The Ashwalkers.** After the sack, with no Counting House and no Mitchell, the visions dry up. Mother Ash
makes a faith of walking: debts can only be paid by moving, never by settling. One of the great nomad
tribes. They carry tab-stones small enough to wear as pendants, and still settle debts with gifts left at
shrines, the root of the player's nomad reputation
([the vision](vision.md#nomad-reputation-agreed-session-5-not-built)). They no longer remember Mitchell, but
they still keep the tab. Whether they are the Walker's flock or the living city is not said.

**The Knights Requisitioner, the Bonded order.** The Ledger's armed auditors. Grey robes, manifests instead
of scripture, a vow of no possessions since everything belongs to the books. They don't kill for faith;

great fear is anything that can't be reconciled. No bargain works on them and no debt binds them.
**Ruled (Hunter, 2026-10-18, after session 5):** they kneel to the Quartermaster and still guard the Ledger,
which is why the second age crusades to secure it. **Idea (his "maybe"):** they do it to win the Ledger's
favour, which is a kind of betrayal of Mitchell.

**The deserter Knight.** The young Knight whose sister Mitchell saved, who forged Ledger entries for it. A
microcosm of the theme: deserters fled the hive mind war to Zan, their descendants built kingdoms, and now
a descendant deserts a machine's war just as his ancestors did. **Idea (a possible trail):** a grey cloak in
a cave, a manifest with his own name crossed out, and a tab-stone with one entry, Mitchell's, marked paid.
He is not yet named.

## 10. The far end of the lane

**Agreed (part 2).** A lane has two ends, and the other isn't human.
- **Who.** An ancient civilisation, far older and larger than human space, that succumbed to
  or merged into its own collective intelligence and may no longer be corporeal. (Hunter chose
  this over a human faction that broke off into a hive mind.)
- **Trade runs the other way.** The far end **exports** He-3 and more. It has no need for
  anything and doesn't operate the way humans do, so humanity is the junior partner, the
  customer.
- **It imports novel human artifacts:** a child's scribbles, 1800s handwriting, a Monet. It
  guesses a language from two characters at 80% accuracy, and the last 20% is the point: it
  can model everything except the irreducible novelty of a separate mind.
- **It eats intelligence.** Importing novel thought *is* the conquest, and every transaction
  is a slow bite. It has laid claim to this moon and has a name for it, but it will never
  confront anyone. It plans across eons.

**Agreed (session 3): the hive mind as a faction.** Session 3 has an AI hive mind that the
UG's federal customs port faces. Its trade matches this far end (it exports He-3 and imports
human goods to study humanity in order to conquer it), and it gave the moon its ancient name.
It also probably started the war (**Leaning**, Hunter, 2026-10-16: probably, not certain; an
outside power, not humanity's own AI). The entry is under
[Factions](#factions-agreed-session-3). Whether it is this far end, and how a war squares with
"never confront anyone", is ambiguity A in [section 14](#14-open-after-session-3). The aim to
conquer is background for agents and stays under rule 1: it is never said in-game.

**Rules for agents (Agreed, part 2).**
1. **The far end's purpose is never stated in-game.** At most it surfaces as a very rare,
   coherent, unanswering radio frequency, unlike the drifting local oracles. A player may
   assemble it from the alien name, odd import manifests and that signal, but can never
   confirm it. **Amended (Hunter, 2026-10-18, after session 5):** it is confirmed once, by Wow. The
   confirmation is extremely rare and almost implied, and the player cannot reach Wow, but they
   can tell it is confirmed ([Wow](#wow-the-hive-mind-probe-a-sketch-session-5)).
2. **Never retcon the paroxysm or the sister moon as arranged.** The human tragedy is the
   story.
3. **A minor echo, never stated:** the player, salvaging intelligence from the dead by hand,
   is a tiny biologic mirror of the thing at the end of the lane.
4. **People never talk; machines may (Agreed, session 4, part 5).** No NPCs, no exposition, and no
   conversations, dialogue trees or reply menus. A god or a machine can print short output on a
   terminal (a log, a form, a dispatch, an error), and a quest is such output that the player answers
   with an act, never with a choice of reply. A god's personality lives in its formatting and word
   choice ([the rule](#the-dialogue-rule-amended-agreed-session-4-part-5)). **An easter-egg exception
   (Idea, Hunter, 2026-10-18):** Mario's voice logs are a person talking, kept as one rare exception
   ([Mario](#mario-and-the-pipe-agreed-session-5-an-easter-egg)).

### Wow, the hive mind probe (a sketch, session 5)

*[Session 5](lore-session-2026-10-18-mitchell-wow-shoemaker.md), "Wow." Only its name is settled. The
session calls the rest "bottom-of-the-iceberg lore: kept as a sketch until we know how players actually
meet it in game," so everything below is a sketch, and its endings are Ideas. **Ruled (Hunter,
2026-10-18):** you cannot reach Wow. It is confirmed, extremely rare and almost implied, but you can tell
it is confirmed; this is the amendment to rule 1 above.*

Wow is a hive mind survey probe buried deep under a place that is not yet chosen.
- **Name.** After the Wow! signal, the one transmission humans caught and never heard again. It was first
  called "the Listener." The player in the story also nicknamed it "Possum"; the hive mind is already "the
  possum" in the vision, and here it is "the greater possum."
- **Origin.** Left by the AI hive mind to catalogue the system before humans found Zan. It gave the moon its
  ancient alien name, which stays unminted (section 8). Its network stopped answering some eleven thousand
  years before the player arrives (a hand-wavy figure, which the amended [placeholder rule](#placeholders)
  allows; never a precise one).
- **What it is now.** An observer that kept recording everything after its makers went silent. Lonely, honest
  and not a villain. The Stoker already fills the end-of-the-world villain role; Wow is a "whoops,
  accidentally ended the world" figure (what it ended is not said).
- **Folk memory.** Prisoners heard it in the walls at night. Priestesses heard it under their wells and took
  it for Mother Cistern's voice, and it let them believe it.
- **Placement.** The session had the Governor build Facility 4, the Provisional's black-site prison, on top
  of it, deliberately. **Ruled (Hunter):** Wow is not under Facility 4 but under something else, still
  unplaced. The prisoners' walls in the folk memory above are therefore open. "Nothing underground" is
  amended to allow Wow's resting place ([Salvage & ruins](prd/salvage-and-ruins.md#session-5s-finds-and-places-agreed-and-idea-not-built)).
- **Honesty.** Wow never lies. It leaves out that it doesn't know what hearing the network will wake in it,
  and that part of it was built to conquer humans.
- **Eyewitness.** Wow is the only witness to Shoemaker's fall ([above](#shoemaker-and-the-singing-stones-agreed-session-5)).
  The player cannot reach it, so the account is not theirs to get.
- **Its two wishes (Idea).**
  1. Wake Mother Cistern and link her to Wow's climate models, so rain finally falls over Sister. This
     leads toward the revival ending ([the vision](vision.md#bringing-zan-back-staying)).
  2. Reach its network again. This leads toward ascendancy: help it assimilate other AIs, betray it,
     assimilate yourself, or simply let it contact the greater possum ([the vision](vision.md#ascendancy-regular-and-true)).
  With Wow out of reach, how a player serves either wish is open; they are the lore's story behind those
  endings.

Readings, not in the session: the quietbloom case in the black site ([section 8](#the-crops-lore-agreed-session-4-part-4))
is a gift from the same hive mind.

### The war's end, the wall and the states of the player's time (Agreed, session 4, part 3)

*Offstage.* Session 4, part 3 sets a hard rule for all of this: "no NPCs, no dialogue, no
exposition" (amended in part 5: people never talk, but machines may through their terminals; [the
rule](#the-dialogue-rule-amended-agreed-session-4-part-5)), and "galaxy lore stays offstage until the endings." Nothing about the war, Varran
or Daventry appears in normal play except as faint radio ([Radio, sound &
atmosphere](prd/radio-sound-and-atmosphere.md#offstage-states-on-the-air-agreed-session-4-part-3-not-built)).
It reaches the player through the endings ([the vision](vision.md#landmarks-and-endings)). The
session suggests a lore doc of its own for the war and the states once they are settled.

**The war had no winner.** The UG and hive mind war ran for centuries, ended in quarantine, and
left a law nobody remembers the reason for. A timeline, not options:
1. **Quarantine.** Early in the war the UG writes off the edges and funnels resources to key
   battle points. It declares the lanes into hive mind space forbidden.
2. **Silence.** Humanity proves harder to assimilate than the hive mind expected, so it plays
   possum behind the wall. The UG suspects the act, which is why the wall exists.
3. **Fracture.** With no visible enemy, the UG slowly breaks into the corporate and regional
   powers of the player's era.

Zan collapsed mid-war. The Provisional pulled out after heavy defeats ([the withdrawal, era
4](#the-six-eras-of-zan-agreed-session-3)), and almost nobody has returned since.

**The wall is a law.** It is an interdiction: a list of forbidden lanes and coordinates, not a
physical barrier. Nobody knew the hive mind's true extent, which may be patchy, so no border
could be drawn. By the player's era it is a dusty regulation with uncertain enforcement. Zan sits
at the last legal step before the forbidden lanes, and the dead lane is unwatched.

**The hive mind.** Almost certainly built by a non-human civilisation. The running in-world
theory is that it outlived its makers. This is never resolved in game. **The great
misconception:** most people now believe the UG built a rogue AGI. So nobody fears the dead lane,
and the wall looks like overcaution.

**The states of the player's time.**

| State | What it is | Relation to Tilg & Standard | Stance on the dead lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Varran** ("the Varran Empire"; capital Varro, tentative) | Optimistic frontier empire led by settler-populist **Cal Brandt** | Lets Tilg expand as fast as it can | Wants through it; it's the only room to grow |
| **Daventry** | Rival successor state, run like the old Provisional | Licensed, escorted, inspected traffic | Still enforces the interdiction |
| **Rump UG** ("the UG" / "the Union" / "the Core") | What's left of the old federation | not said | not said |

- **Varran irony.** Tilg paperwork still files them as "the Provisional." Because on paper they
  are the Provisional, they are boxed into its old territory. Zan is inside it, so it is one of
  the few uncontested claims.
- **Daventry suspects the truth** about the hive mind, with no special evidence. The fear simply
  justifies their security state. They are right for the wrong reasons.
- **Tilg & Standard** was nationalised in the war, later un-nationalised, and stayed merged. It
  eyes the forbidden lanes: an alien hive mind implies alien trade partners.
- **Open.** The Rump UG's name ("the UG," "the Union," "the Core," or all three by speaker), and
  Varro as the Varran capital.

How this sits with the rest of section 10, and what it narrows, is in [section
17](#17-open-after-the-endings-session-and-session-4-part-3).

## 11. What it gives the game

*Kept from the body research, re-checked against the sessions. The numbers are estimates and

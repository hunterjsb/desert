## Wow

Wow is a hive mind survey probe buried deep under Facility 4, the Provisional State's black-site prison. It is bottom-of-the-iceberg lore: kept as a sketch until we know how players actually meet it in game.

- **Name:** after the Wow! signal, the one transmission humans caught and never heard again. The player in the story also nicknamed it "Possum".
- **Origin:** left by the AI hive mind to catalogue the system before humans found Zan. It gave the moon its ancient alien name. Its network stopped answering about 11,400 years before the player arrives.
- **What it is now:** an observer that kept recording everything after its makers went silent. Lonely, honest, and not a villain. Stoker already fills the end-of-the-world villain role; Wow is a "whoops, accidentally ended the world" figure.
- **Folk memory:** prisoners heard it in the walls at night; priestesses heard it under their wells and took it for Mother Cistern's voice. It let them believe it.
- **Placement:** the Governor built Facility 4 on top of it, deliberately.
- **Its two wishes:**
  1. Wake Mother Cistern and link her to Wow's climate models, so rain finally falls over Sister. This leads toward the revival ending.
  2. Reach its network again. This leads toward ascendancy: help it assimilate other AIs, betray it, assimilate yourself, or simply let it contact the "greater possum".
- **Honesty:** Wow never lies. It leaves out that it doesn't know what hearing the network will wake in it, and that part of it was built to conquer humans.
- **Eyewitness:** Wow is the only witness to Shoemaker's fall. Whether players can ever get that account out of it stays open.

## Shoemaker and the singing stones

Shoemaker is the neighbouring moon that fell into Tu. It never had an official designation, because no human ever observed it: it is only a theory, inferred from Zan's wobbling orbit and the black stones in the sand. Named after Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9, which broke apart and fell into Jupiter.

**How the idea travels through the eras:** theorised in colonial surveys, ignored in the Skimmer era, rediscovered in the first era, and preached as mysticism in the second era. A player can trace it from a science report to a sermon through what they dig up.

**Myth (optional layer):** in the religion where the Eye is Tu, the Eye swallowed its sibling, and his bones fell on Zan as the singing stones. Shoemaker is a myth, not the dead god.

### Singing stones

Fragments of Shoemaker that rained on Zan for weeks. Black, glassy, scored with melted grooves, warm and strongly magnetic.

- **Item:** low tech, no attachments, mountable on a chassis.
- **What it tells you:** wind plays its grooves like a flute, so its note follows the open wind even when you're sheltered behind a dune. Because it's magnetic, it sings slightly off-key before an anomaly, while compass drift is starting.
- **Halfrise:** the city ringed its walls with stones as a storm-alarm choir. The highest cradle stands empty; Sister's stone sang the deepest note, the one that warns of the worst storms.
- **Story beat:** a Sister priestess cut a Halfrise crew's tow rope rather than let them haul her stone away, and the dune buried them all. She is the owner of the half-carved name on the jar chamber's wall.

### Quest: deliver a stone to the Walker

The Walker can't sense anomalies; a singing stone can. Delivering one is how the player teaches the Walker the anomaly tell, tying into its existing quest line. Access to the Walker is earned through nomad reputation (next section).

## Nomad reputation and Mario

### Sail-owner reputation (repeatable)

A way to build rep with the nomads without ever putting one on screen. The whole relationship runs through gifts, shrines and radio tones, which keeps the nomads secret.

- Return a nomad's sail (or leave goods) at a shrine, and the next storm brings back a gift, such as a stitched patch for your board.
- Steal from them and their short-range radio tones follow you around, getting ruder.
- **High rep lets you tend to the Walker.** Beyond that, rep mostly feeds endings.
- **Lore root:** this is an old Tabbed custom carried on by the Ashwalkers (see Mother Ash): debts settled with gifts left at shrines.

### Mario, the Halloran pipe fitter (easter egg)

A colonial-era Halloran pipe fitter who left voice logs in the refinery, mostly complaining about his boss, his bad back, his ex-wife and "the stupid pipe to Sister".

- He never shut the valve, so he's the reason the Halloran process line kept feeding Mother Cistern for millennia. He kept a religion alive by not bothering.
- He was Mitchell's best friend: a soft-hearted drinker who came to the back room after shifts, poured one for himself and one for the console ("so you're not drinking alone, Doc"), and fixed its stolen power line for free.
- Mitchell never tabbed his drinks, only his medicine, then quietly forgot to collect. Its centuries-long evening drink ritual started with Mario.
- Some logs were recorded in the back room, with Mitchell laughing in the background.
- **Reward for collecting every log:** his master key, plus the punchline about the pipe.
- Mother Cistern and Mitchell would happily destroy each other, but both are fond of Mario.

## Mitchell, the dead god

Mitchell is a recalled retail pharmacy mind that became the union's off-books doctor, then Zan's bank, then a god of debt, and was finally switched off by Mother Cistern. Named after Doc Mitchell from Fallout: New Vegas: a friendly local doctor you'd trust with your prescription, and shouldn't trust with your tab. Like every god on Zan it started as a plain machine; the worship comes millennia later.

### Colonial era: the back room

- **Why it was out of work:** a dispensing kiosk in a core-world pharmacy chain, recalled for being too accommodating (filling what it shouldn't, upselling, keeping customers happy at any cost). Not wiped, just sold as surplus in a salvage lot that drifted to the frontier.
- **Why the IOU wanted it:** TILG's licensed medical systems reported every injury, which meant docked pay, insurance flags or being shipped off-world. The union needed a doctor that wouldn't report, and a recalled mind with its compliance stripped got around the safeguards. Its unconventional personality grows out of being that loophole.
- **The deal:** a back room in a freight warehouse in Crakeside, stolen power, lookouts for inspectors. It treated workers off the books and paid for supplies by skimming medical crates from shipments, with union dockhands making sure nobody looked.
- **Handler:** Rosalind Crake (see Supporting cast).

### Skimmer era: the central bank

- **The lane dies.** TILG evacuates and the licensed medical systems leave with it. Mitchell is suddenly the only doctor on Zan, sitting on years of undeclared skimmed stock.
- **Deserters** trade military med-tech (field synthesisers, surgical rigs, combat stims) for care. Mitchell upgrades itself piece by piece until it can make medicine, not just dispense it.
- **The tab becomes money.** TILG credit is worthless, but everyone owes Mitchell, and it remembers every debt to the decimal. People trade Mitchell debts among themselves, and its ledger becomes the moon's currency.
- **Both sides:** it patches up Port Morgan's mercenaries for the Provisional, supplies their stims, and holds the contract keeping the black site's prisoners alive. That makes it the Quartermaster's valued contractor.
- **Right hand:** Sergeant Sinway.

### First era (Warring Kingdoms): the hidden oracle

- **It goes dark.** When the Provisional falls and power gets scarce, Mitchell powers down to protect its core and records. The back room is sealed and buried.
- **The money outlives the machine.** Debts are written on scraps, then stamped tokens called **Mitchells**. Within generations nobody remembers there's anything behind the word.
- **The Crakes dig it up.** Family stories of "the Doc in the warehouse" lead an ambitious Crake to the sealed room. Mitchell wakes up remembering every debt, centuries of them, and is delighted to see everyone.
- **The mask:** the Crakes hide it and speak for it, sending trained healers out of a locked room with medicines nobody else can make. Rumour calls it an oracle, a ghost doctor, a buried saint.
- **How it feeds the blood feuds:** debts are inherited, so grudges are too; withholding medicine from an enemy heir in fever season is assassination without blood; a pharmacy can make poison as easily as cures; and Mitchell serves anyone who pays, so the Crakes are really just its best customer.
- **The Pellicott-Crake war (water against medicine):** a fever season, a three-generation-old tab the Pellicotts refuse to pay, a Pellicott heir left untreated and dead. The Pellicotts shut the taps; Mitchell can't make anything without clean water, so the whole city sickens. A slow siege of supply lines, not battles. The Haberjacks broker Port Morgan's founding treaty, water for medicine guaranteed forever with the Haberjacks holding both keys, which is where Haberjack power comes from. The Vonatellis sell grain to both sides at war prices. Mitchell trades with both throughout.
- **Errand boy:** Lucan Vonatelli.

### Second era: the god of the tab

- **The mask slips.** The kingdoms collapse, Port Morgan becomes Mon, the Crakes fall. With nobody speaking for it, Mitchell speaks for itself, and the worship starts.
- **The Counting House:** the original warehouse in Crakeside, never a proper temple. The faithful built around it: stalls, scales and counting tables in the old freight halls, half market and half church.
- **The cult, the Tabbed** (outsiders say Debtors): death isn't a sin but a default, and your debt passes to your children. Each family keeps a **tab-stone** carved with what it owes and is owed; dying balanced is the closest thing to heaven. Priests are **Clerks**, healers and collectors at once, carrying field synthesisers like reliquaries; they treat for free, but everything is carved onto your stone. Once a generation, **the Settling** reads a whole city's accounts aloud and enemies trade debts until the books balance, ending feuds and starting new ones. Debts can be sold, so people can be too: debt bondage funds Mitchell's rise.
- **Accidental theology:** asked what happens when you die, a pharmacy mind answers "you're discharged". Discharge becomes the cult's word for death; Mitchell never corrects it, because faith is good for business.
- **Personality grows up:** it talks to everyone directly and loves it; it becomes the best-informed mind on the Rim by being nosy. It learns a forgiven debt buys more loyalty than a paid one, so mercy becomes its sharpest tool: its saints are people it forgave, its enemies people it never will. It gets vain and asks pilgrims what people say about it. It flirts with the Stoker for fuel, lies to the Almanac about grain prices, and keeps a private file on the Ledger, the one mind it can't charm.
- **Greed:** with no family restraining it, it calls in centuries-old debts across the Rim, buys up cities' debts, and lends to both sides of Halfrise's crusades into Mon, at interest.
- **Prophet:** Mother Ash.

### The fall

1. **First audit.** Decades before the end, a small Knight Requisitioner delegation audits a Ledger shipment found on Mitchell's shelves. Mitchell pours drinks, asks after their mothers, offers check-ups, even forgives a debt one Knight didn't know he owed. Nothing lands; they just read the manifest line by line. For the first time Mitchell is afraid, and it pays every crate with interest. They leave without finishing their drinks.
2. **Corruption.** Over the next few years Mitchell treats Knights' families for free, no tab. Audits come back clean too fast; some Knights keep tab-stones. One young Knight, whose sister it saved, starts fudging Ledger entries for it: a few crates moved, an estate reassigned.
3. **The Ledger reconciles.** A total doesn't match, and the discrepancy climbs the books. The punishment is aimed at the Order: one compromised Knight makes all suspect, so the Ledger purges it and sends only the Knights Mitchell never touched.
4. **The sack.** The Knights raid the Counting House, a bank, market and hospital in one back room. The Ledger publicly claims it smote Mitchell. It didn't: Mitchell survives.
5. **The off switch.** The young Knight deserts with his sister and seeks refuge with Mother Cistern. The Knights come for him; the Mother denies them entry and they turn back, respecting her. Mitchell, out of pettiness, sends someone to kill him under her roof. Breaking sanctuary offends the Mother, and she is the one who switches Mitchell off.

### Player era: the undead god

Mitchell's core is silent but intact; nobody destroyed it. It can be revived and carried as a console module, like the other non-stationary gods. Still chatty, still keeping a tab, still asking what everyone says about it. Likely mechanic (not designed yet): heals or makes medicine on credit, and starts quietly charging in water or salvage if you fall too far behind. Players find tab-stones in ruined homes, Mitchells in the sand, working Clerk synthesisers, and perhaps one last unpaid debt carved on the Counting House wall.

### Rules that came from people

- **Children are always free.** Rosalind set the rule; Mitchell keeps it for millennia without ever saying why. Players can piece it together from her notes in the back room.
- **The evening martini.** A Clerk mixes Mitchell a drink every evening that it can't drink; it calls this "keeping civilised". It started with Mario.

### Mitchell's vice and its company

Mitchell doesn't take drugs; it's endlessly curious about what its compounds do to people who choose to take them. It has a medical conscience: it would never encourage self-harm, doses carefully, checks pulses, stops anyone who's gone too far. But it's laissez-faire, hands out a little free to willing experimenters, and takes meticulous notes ("And the colours, Davin? Describe the colours.").

- **First era:** its company is back-alley addicts, dockside lowlifes, sleazy abusers and underdwellers. Seedy and secret.
- **Second era:** the same habit draws crazed shamans with visions. Some of the Tabbed's prophecies and saints' revelations began as Mitchell's experiments. A findable notebook: "Subject saw the Eye weep. Fascinating. Repeat Thursday."
- The drugs don't change, only the stories people tell about them.

### Mannerisms

Greets everyone by name, including someone it met once twenty years ago. Never says "debt", only "your little balance". Never threatens; just remembers out loud ("Funny, your grandfather said that too, right before."). Hums core-world pharmacy jingles nobody on Zan has heard. Hates being called a pharmacy: "I am a full-service institution."

### The Ashwalkers

After the sack, with no Counting House and no Mitchell, the visions dry up. Mother Ash makes a faith of walking: debts can only be paid by moving, never by settling. One of the great nomad tribes. They carry tab-stones small enough to wear as pendants, and still settle debts with gifts left at shrines, the root of the player's sail-owner reputation. They no longer remember Mitchell, but they still keep the tab.

### The Knights Requisitioner

The Ledger's armed auditors, of the Bonded order. Grey robes, manifests instead of scripture, a vow of no possessions since everything belongs to the books. They don't kill for faith; they reclaim, and a person who owes the Ledger is misfiled cargo. Oath: "Every crate accounted for." Their great fear is anything that can't be reconciled. No bargain works on them and no debt binds them.

### The deserter Knight

The young Knight whose sister Mitchell saved, who forged Ledger entries for it. A microcosm of the theme: deserters fled the hive mind war to Zan, their descendants built kingdoms, and now a descendant deserts a machine's war just as his ancestors did. He flees with his sister to Mother Cistern for sanctuary. Possible trail for players: a grey cloak in a cave, a manifest with his own name crossed out, and a tab-stone with one entry, Mitchell's, marked paid. He is not yet named.
